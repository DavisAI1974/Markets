# Independent reanalysis of the four NG days (October 2, 3, 17, 18, 2023) — Claude Code

Written 2026-11-09 against the frozen common packet (markets-data e7b1c4a, `shared-reanalysis-inputs/final-42593665910-1`) and the shared brief at Markets 2781ad1 (including the November 9 price-as-input addition). This is one of two independent reports; by the user's instruction it is not reconciled with Codex's. Companion files: CLAIMS.csv, CASES.csv, COVERAGE.csv, CONFLICTS.csv, DIPOLE_LAYER_MAP.csv, OPEN_QUESTIONS.md, PAPER_STRATEGIES.md/.json, PAPER_READINESS.md, RECEIPT.json, and the scripts (`scripts/m*.py`) and tables (`tables/`) that produced every number below. All numbers are gross; no execution assumption enters any of them. Each date is reported separately; nothing is pooled across dates as a headline.

## 1. What is useful (lead)

1. **The Native dipole is recognizable only after it is half over.** The actual recognition clock arrives 51.7 to 52.3 s (median) after the birth second. Under the operational lifetime (third consecutive opposite second), the lifecycle had already ended at or before recognition for 383/741, 456/832, 383/740 and 432/863 live candidates (0.50 to 0.55 on every date). For the rest, the remaining life has median 43 to 51 s and p90 187 to 260 s. The recorded flow state at emission sorts this strongly: "opposite" candidates were already dead in 0.93 to 0.96 of cases, "same" in 0.46 to 0.51, "undefined" in 0.26 to 0.33 (CLAIMS M1-1, M1-2).
2. **At recognition the dipole carries no direction.** Oriented 300 s midpoint moves after recognition are up/down 337/349, 379/341, 293/367, 423/362 (Ccode's midpoint construct) and 305/323, 333/362, 280/353, 354/382 (last-print construct); the same holds inside SAME, FLIP, every flow state, every touch relation and (Oct 18) every pre-recognition drift class (M1-3, M1-4). The pre-recognition drift in the birth direction that both earlier passes documented is real on Oct 18 (382 with, 278 against by recognition) but it does not continue.
3. **The one recurring, actionable price pattern in the packet is the return after a large midpoint jump.** Jumps of at least 2 ticks extend in the first 5 s (extreme median 3 to 4.5 ticks) and then return fully to the pre-move quote within 600 s in 35/46, 48/58, 42/57, 49/64 cases; median return 38, 9, 14, 84 s (M2-1). This is the basis of the single paper candidate marked ready for an execution check (S2). Prior pressure signals, jump size and print count do not sort which moves return.
4. **Short-horizon direction lives in book layers, not in the dipole, and decays within a minute.** Touch imbalance, recent aggressor flow and refill at the hit side give 0.54 to 0.61 continuation at 1 s on all four dates, falling to 0.48 to 0.53 by 60 to 300 s (M4-1..3, M5-1, M8b-3). Thin levels break or hold predictably at the millisecond scale (standing best shrank under attacking fresh flow retreats 0.63 to 0.66; a new one-lot best with flow going the other way holds 0.61 to 0.67) (M3-1).
5. **Price changes are followed by more activity but not by a directional book response.** In the second after a midpoint change, adds, cancels, fills and prints roughly double relative to quiet seconds on every date, with no side asymmetry; the same-second events (fills on the side price moved toward, adds on the side left behind) are the price change itself (M8-1, M8-2). Once the mechanical next-second quote move is excluded, the book's +1 s response carries no information about the next 20 s (M8-4). The next midpoint change is a reversal more often than a continuation on every date and in both populations (M8-3), and two consecutive same-direction changes reverse even more often (section 6).
6. **Earlier recognition exists but is noisy.** Weakening onsets of the previous dipole precede the next birth by 12 to 32 s (median) with onset-to-birth precision 0.38 to 0.65; flow_vanished and flow_resumed are the most precise (0.58 to 0.65) (M6-1). No price outcome after onsets is measured in the packet.

## 2. Method and rationale (independently chosen)

I treated the question as one of **decision-clock conditional distributions on whole lifecycles**: for each source population, fix the earliest clock at which the information exists (recognition receive time for Native dipoles; cutoff = start − 1 ns for family starts; onset known-by time for thin-touch episodes and pressure signals; move known-by time for large moves), take only the prefix available at that clock, and tabulate later outcomes as up/flat/down counts and quantiles per date. I never averaged across dates and never used a mean as the headline. Where a measured comparison already existed in the suite (Tier 1 and Tier 2 cross-day tables, four-arm metrics, prebirth contrasts, continuous precursor summary), I read the tables rather than rerunning the suite. Where a needed outcome did not exist (post-recognition price path for all four dates, remaining life relative to the actual clock, per-second responses after price changes), I computed it from the underlying frozen records (whole-lifecycle parquet, native per-second paths, a-trades prints, combined-events parquet) and saved the per-row tables. For the price-as-input direction I used the native per-second paths of both candidates and their matched controls as two populations (dipole-conditioned and general), separated same-second (mechanical) from later-second (response) book events, and checked every conditional with a non-overlapping outcome window. Controls were used as the general-market sample, not as a significance device.

Populations kept distinct throughout: archived Native candidates 741/833/741/863 (3,178; two finalization-only), their 6,352 matched controls, first-observed structural families 795/848/823/749 (764 universe on Oct 18), Ccode's independent detections 839/857/830/890, 22,042/24,349/27,111/27,551 thin-touch episodes, 109,307/116,187/114,994/109,754 pressure signals, 46/58/57/64 large moves, 600k to 654k completed groups per day. Oct 2 and Oct 17 are partial sessions.

## 4. Behavior preceding price: what becomes recognizable, when, and from which sequence

**Native dipole (M1).** The birth second is the start (62 to 68% of births) or an early second of a fully one-sided 20 s window; nothing in the packet makes the birth knowable before the recognition clock. At that clock the knowable facts are: polarity (tautological with the window), orientation, the flow state at emission, the lag class, and whether the window has already gone undefined (no prints for 20 s). The last two sort remaining life: undefined-before-recognition gives remaining life p50 48 to 59 s versus 23 to 30 s otherwise; lag above 56 s (187 to 235 candidates per day) goes with lifecycles of 80 to 94 s median and only 0.33 to 0.39 already ended (a selection effect of long one-sided runs), lag 49 to 56 s with 31 to 40 s lifecycles and 0.59 to 0.68 ended. None of this sorts direction.

**Families (M4, M8b).** At a first-observed structural family start the full displayed book is bid-heavy at 718/795, 847/848, 754/823, 749/749 starts: this is a standing property of these sessions, not a start signal (OPEN_QUESTIONS 9). Oriented to the signed 5 s flow, the next 1 s continues in 0.56/0.55/0.56/0.61 of 664/679/687/573 families, 5 s in 0.53/0.53/0.53/0.56, 60 s in 0.53/0.48/0.51/0.53, 300 s in 0.53/0.52/0.48/0.50. Oriented to touch imbalance, 1 s 0.58/0.54/0.54/0.59, 20 s 0.55/0.56/0.52/0.57, 300 s 0.49/0.53/0.52/0.51. Before a start there is more classified activity than at matched controls in paired counts on every date and lead (total20 greater-minus-less +128/+90/+89/+24 at 1 s), while the mean differences flip sign on Oct 3 depending on control weighting (CONFLICTS C7), so the paired counts, not the means, are the claim. The ridge arms agree in direction 0.33 to 0.54 of the time; none exceed 0.55 anywhere.

**Thin touches and pressure signals (M3).** The episode population is large and the conditioning is clean (strictly prior flow, onset known-by clock). Retreat share by onset type is 0.37 to 0.42 for a new one-lot best and 0.59 to 0.63 for a standing best that shrank; with attacking 1 s flow the shrank case retreats 0.63 to 0.66 on both sides and all dates, with flow going away the new-best case holds 0.61 to 0.67. The durations are 1.2 ms median, so this is a statement about how the book works rather than a trade. The delayed 1/5/30 s midpoint outcomes after size-one onsets show an up bias on both sides and all dates (up 0.34 to 0.38 vs down 0.19 to 0.24 at 1 s) that I could not reconcile with symmetric day baselines (midpoint up/down 16456/16558, 18148/17936, 18344/18566, 17745/17877 at group level); until the source's sign convention is checked (CONFLICTS C9) it stays an observation.

**Grid couplings (M5).** Across 2,669 condition/outcome pairs, 93 book-response pairs are beyond every day's shifted-Y line (activity at the touch persists), plus spread → next spread change and quote-change count → next absolute move; signed-move pairs repeat in sign (touch imbalance → dm_5 +0.11 to +0.14) but are never beyond the line on more than one day. Simultaneity dominates at 1 s: midpoint change and touch-size change co-move at lag 0 (|ρ| 0.53 to 0.58), and the only lead is touch imbalance → flow by one second.

**Early warning (M6).** Onset → next-birth precision and birth coverage per date are in tables/m5_m6_summary.json; the directional outcome after an onset is near symmetric in the source's outcome summary (follows versus opposes within a few percent).

**Ccode's independent population (M7).** On a different detector the same message appears: O, S and X seeds confirm exhaustion before a 50 s information horizon in 0.82 to 0.94 of cases on every date, P seeds never (0 of 249/242/268/265); remaining runway at the horizon is −24 to −28 s (O, S, X) versus +65 to +73 s (P). The a-state known at +60 s does not sort 300 or 600 s direction (A-fast-collapse d300 171/179, 202/189, 156/184, 198/196; A-persistent 178/187, 151/164, 130/228, 183/186). Time to a 3-tick move with the dipole direction is minutes (S seeds 195 to 379 s median, P seeds 767 to 1,373 s). Chain plays are too few to read (8/6/15/14).

## 5. Price as an input: how price changes are followed by orders, liquidity, trading and later price

Population: every second in which the midpoint changed inside a native path (candidate paths −60 s to recognition; control paths anchored at matched arbitrary times): 5,481/5,716/5,878/7,296 candidate seconds and 5,954 to 7,851 per control set, almost all 1-tick changes.

**Same second (mechanical).** Fills are on the side price moved toward (toward>away in 3,309/3,493/3,417/4,079 candidate seconds versus 445/476/440/513 the other way), cancels on that side, adds on the side left behind. These events are the price change; nothing here is a response.

**Seconds +1, +2 to +5, +6 to +20 (response).** Activity roughly doubles: adds on both sides 19.6/20.4/18.9/16.0 per second at +1 versus 5.8/6.4/6.3/6.2 in quiet seconds; 51/59/55/48 over +2..+5 versus 26/29/29/26; 164/193/185/151 over +6..+20 versus 100/108/106/95 (candidate paths; controls show the same ratios). Fills and prints double likewise. But the extra activity has no side: toward-side versus away-side adds over +2..+5 are 2,040/2,279, 2,097/2,513, 2,271/2,265, 2,841/2,896. The response to a price change on these days is more trading and more quoting on both sides, not replenishment on one side and withdrawal on the other. Whether this is reaction or the same activity cluster that produced the move cannot be separated at 1 s (OPEN_QUESTIONS 11).

**Later price.** The next 5 s midpoint change is a reversal more often than a continuation on every date and both populations (candidate up/down 982/1,315, 1,024/1,365, 1,127/1,516, 1,326/1,824; control 1,349/1,561, 1,361/1,747, 1,447/1,980, 1,686/2,251), as expected from a 1 to 2 tick spread. After a second same-direction change inside +1 s, the +1..+20 s move reverses even more often (candidate 82/123, 112/140, 105/160, 115/200; controls 127/188, 166/237, 167/234, 257/334). After an immediate bounce, the +1..+20 s move is symmetric.

**Does the response predict the later price?** With overlapping windows it appears to: adds on the attacked side in +2..+5 → 20 s reversal 838 vs 408 up (Oct 2 candidate) and similarly on every date. With the outcome measured from +5 to +20 the association shrinks to 569/494, 575/513, 650/622, 736/726 against 652/540, 670/594, 661/540, 809/691 for the opposite conditioning, i.e. both reversal-leaning by about the same amount. Conditioning on +1 s events among seconds whose quote did not move in +1 s gives 290/330 vs 286/336 (adds toward vs away, Oct 2) and the same near-symmetry on Oct 3, 17, 18 and in the controls. The apparent "read the replenishment, then trade the reversal" relationship is the reversal itself, observed in the same second. This is the main caution for any feedback-based rule.

**Trading follows quote changes immediately.** The completed group after a midpoint-moving group carries a print in 0.35 to 0.38 of cases versus 0.03 to 0.04 after a flat group, with the next print 0.3 to 0.4 ms later; 0.36 to 0.39 even when the moving group itself had no print. At message speed this is either a sweep continuing or algorithmic reaction; it is not separable here.

**Large moves (price → book → price).** Half of the 2-tick jumps continue in the first 5 s and half reverse; the extreme is reached inside 5 s in most cases; 74 to 83% return fully within 600 s. The return is the clearest price-driven behavior in the packet, and it is the same mechanism as the 1-tick bounce at larger scale.

**Thin-level endings (mechanics of price through a level).** Retreats are produced by trades through the level in 0.56 to 0.63 of cases and by pulled quotes in 0.37 to 0.44; recoveries are replenishment in essentially all cases (fill-linked 1/0/0/0).

**Price → flow at family starts (Oct 18 only).** Flow at the start agrees with the prior 120 s price direction 0.62 to 0.67 of the time (flow follows price on this clock); the prior direction's continuation is 0.58 at 1 s and 0.47 at 60 to 300 s.

**Grid evidence.** Recent quote-change count (300 s) predicts adds and cancels at both touches over the next 5 s (0.16 to 0.22) and the next 20 s absolute move (0.13 to 0.19) on all four days. This is activity begetting activity and larger moves, not direction.

## 6. Continuation, pause, exhaustion, reversal, remaining duration

Endpoints used: the third-opposite-second convention (dipoles), Ccode's confirm rule, the first full return (large moves), the episode ending (thin touches), fixed horizons (families, grid). None is natural death. Under the dipole convention the median life is 38 to 43 s, p90 178 to 200 s, max 564 to 1,066 s; at the recognition clock the remaining life is as in section 1. Under Ccode's rule the runway is 20 to 25 s for O, S and X seeds and 113 to 121 s for P seeds. For large moves, half-return comes at 0.2 to 12 s median and full return at 9 to 84 s. These three descriptions agree qualitatively (most moves are spent within tens of seconds; a persistent minority lasts minutes) while being different conventions on different populations (CONFLICTS C12). No decision-time feature in the packet separates the persistent minority except the dipole flow state and Ccode's seed class, which are partly definitional.

## 7. Dipole layer and aspect review: sufficiency

DIPOLE_LAYER_MAP.csv maps 18 source-defined layers (fresh and rolling flow, expiry, ratio/polarity/magnitude, birth/nominal/recognition clocks, orientation, flow state at emission, Ccode post-recognition labels, the lifetime convention, precursor predicates, Ccode lean and birth-axis paths, Codex M1/M2 events, Tier 1 operands, Tier 2 conditions, lead-lag series, pressure signals, large moves, Ccode seed states, external layers) with clocks, coverage, incremental evidence, conflicts and gaps.

Decision coverage, as measured on these four days:

- Recognition: yes, late (half the lifecycle gone). Earlier recognition from weakening onsets exists with 0.4 to 0.65 precision.
- Direction: the polarity is a window tautology and has no post-recognition content on any date; the pre-recognition drift is not available at the decision clock.
- Remaining strength and duration: partly, through flow state at emission and the undefined-window state (both partly definitional), and through the lag class.
- Continuation versus failure: not from the dipole; the 1 to 5 s continuation evidence lives in touch imbalance, refill and thin-touch flow (book layers).
- Exhaustion and reversal: yes, but mostly already realised by recognition.
- Adverse-move avoidance: the "opposite at emission" state is an exit cue; no entry cue.
- Executable rules: none from the dipole alone.

Dipole-only versus other data versus combined: the only measured comparison that puts the dipole next to complementary inputs on the same rows is Codex's flow-state cell table at recognition (which sorts the pre-recognition move, not the post-recognition one) and my M1 cells (which show no post-recognition sorting). The Tier 2 forward stack never selects a dipole quantity for signed outcomes (the first conditions are touch imbalance, spread and quote-change counts). So the user's hypothesis that the dipole layers together supply most of the needed knowledge is not supported by these four days: they supply recognition and exhaustion timing of a window that is usually spent, and nothing about direction or magnitude after recognition. What they add that nothing else adds is the fresh-versus-expiring decomposition, which is the correct way to read whether a one-sided run is weakening by new opposition or merely by the clock. The smallest useful follow-up is OPEN_QUESTIONS 7 (onset-based entry tested on price).

## 9. Changes from previous conclusions

Strengthened: the recognition lag structure and the window mechanics (both earlier passes) are confirmed and extended to the remaining-life table; the large-move return (Codex combined study) is confirmed per date with timing distributions; the refill and touch-imbalance 1 to 20 s reads (Ccode Tier 1/2) are confirmed as book-layer, not dipole-layer, content. Weakened: the pre-recognition ramp as a trading object (it does not continue after recognition); any directional reading of SAME/FLIP; the Tier 1 original stack (reversed on Oct 3). New: half of lifecycles end before recognition; price changes are followed by symmetric, non-directional activity bursts; the apparent replenishment→reversal link is the reversal itself; size-one delayed outcomes carry an unexplained up bias (flagged, not used). Unresolved: identity between Ccode's and the archived populations; the origin of the sub-40 s recognition class; why Oct 3 differs.

## 10. Practical limits

Four already-studied days of one instrument; Oct 2 and 17 partial; each day one dependent set; no out-of-sample claim. Message-level data underlies every finding except the large-move return; Tradovate supplies top-of-book and depth snapshots only, so only S2 can be paper-checked tonight (PAPER_READINESS.md). No execution evidence exists in the packet; nothing here is net of anything. The reports are discovery readouts, not validation.
