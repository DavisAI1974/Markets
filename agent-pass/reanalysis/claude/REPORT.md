## 2. Method and rationale (independently chosen)

I treated the question as one of **decision-clock conditional distributions on whole lifecycles**: for each source population, fix the earliest clock at which the information exists (recognition receive time for Native dipoles; cutoff = start − 1 ns for family starts; onset known-by time for thin-touch episodes and pressure signals; move known-by time for large moves), take only the prefix available at that clock, and tabulate later outcomes as up/flat/down counts and quantiles per date. I never averaged across dates and never used a mean as the headline. Where a measured comparison already existed in the suite (Tier 1 and Tier 2 cross-day tables, four-arm metrics, prebirth contrasts, continuous precursor summary), I read the tables rather than rerunning the suite. Where a needed outcome did not exist (post-recognition price path for all four dates, remaining life relative to the actual clock, per-second responses after price changes), I computed it from the underlying frozen records (whole-lifecycle parquet, native per-second paths, a-trades prints, combined-events parquet) and saved the per-row tables. For the price-as-input direction I used the native per-second paths of both candidates and their matched controls as two populations (dipole-conditioned and general), separated same-second (mechanical) from later-second (response) book events, and checked every conditional with a non-overlapping outcome window. Controls were used as the general-market sample, not as a significance device.

Populations kept distinct throughout: archived Native candidates 741/833/741/863 (3,178; two finalization-only), their 6,352 matched controls, first-observed structural families 795/848/823/749 (764 universe on Oct 18), Ccode's independent detections 839/857/830/890, 22,042/24,349/27,111/27,551 thin-touch episodes, 109,307/116,187/114,994/109,754 pressure signals, 46/58/57/64 large moves, 600k to 654k completed groups per day. Oct 2 and Oct 17 are partial sessions.

**Rerun additions.** (a) The raw tapes were decoded with databento 0.90 (DBN MBO schema) and replayed order by order into a book keyed by order ID, emitting end-of-second state and within-second event counts per side (`scripts/m22_raw_mbo_queue_layers.py`). (b) Every dipole mark is dated at the END of the completed second in which it becomes knowable, and the midpoint used for a ride to that mark is the end-of-second midpoint; the start-of-second variant is retained alongside as the first pass's convention (`m16`, `m16b`). (c) The earliest-knowable population is the detector ledger's saturated-second clusters (`crossday/detector/ledger_saturated_seconds_<day>.csv`), priced with the raw book (`m24`). (d) All layers knowable at +5 s, derived and raw, are tested the same way (per-date tertiles, four-date sign consistency, leave-one-date-out score) against three outcomes: the realistic exit (exhaustion-end or 60 s), 60 s and 300 s (`m17`, `m23`, `m25`, `m26`).

## 7. Behavior preceding price: what becomes recognizable, when, and from which sequence

**Native dipole (M1).** The birth second is the start (62 to 68% of births) or an early second of a fully one-sided 20 s window; nothing in the packet makes the birth knowable before the recognition clock. At that clock the knowable facts are: polarity (tautological with the window), orientation, the flow state at emission, the lag class, and whether the window has already gone undefined (no prints for 20 s). The last two sort remaining life: undefined-before-recognition gives remaining life p50 48 to 59 s versus 23 to 30 s otherwise; lag above 56 s (187 to 235 candidates per day) goes with lifecycles of 80 to 94 s median and only 0.33 to 0.39 already ended (a selection effect of long one-sided runs), lag 49 to 56 s with 31 to 40 s lifecycles and 0.59 to 0.68 ended. ## 8. Price as an input: how price changes are followed by orders, liquidity, trading and later price

Population: every second in which the midpoint changed inside a native path (candidate paths −60 s to recognition; control paths anchored at matched arbitrary times): 5,481/5,716/5,878/7,296 candidate seconds and 5,954 to 7,851 per control set, almost all 1-tick changes.

**Same second (mechanical).** Fills are on the side price moved toward (toward>away in 3,309/3,493/3,417/4,079 candidate seconds versus 445/476/440/513 the other way), cancels on that side, adds on the side left behind. These events are the price change; nothing here is a response.

**Seconds +1, +2 to +5, +6 to +20 (response).** Activity roughly doubles: adds on both sides 19.6/20.4/18.9/16.0 per second at +1 versus 5.8/6.4/6.3/6.2 in quiet seconds; 51/59/55/48 over +2..+5 versus 26/29/29/26; 164/193/185/151 over +6..+20 versus 100/108/106/95 (candidate paths; controls show the same ratios). Fills and prints double likewise. But the extra activity has no side: toward-side versus away-side adds over +2..+5 are 2,040/2,279, 2,097/2,513, 2,271/2,265, 2,841/2,896. The response to a price change on these days is more trading and more quoting on both sides, not replenishment on one side and withdrawal on the other. Whether this is reaction or the same activity cluster that produced the move cannot be separated at 1 s (OPEN_QUESTIONS 11).

**Later price.** The next 5 s midpoint change is a reversal more often than a continuation on every date and both populations (candidate up/down 982/1,315, 1,024/1,365, 1,127/1,516, 1,326/1,824; control 1,349/1,561, 1,361/1,747, 1,447/1,980, 1,686/2,251), as expected from a 1 to 2 tick spread. After a second same-direction change inside +1 s, the +1..+20 s move reverses even more often (candidate 82/123, 112/140, 105/160, 115/200; controls 127/188, 166/237, 167/234, 257/334). After an immediate bounce, the +1..+20 s move is symmetric.

**Does the response predict the later price?** With overlapping windows it appears to: adds on the attacked side in +2..+5 → 20 s reversal 838 vs 408 up (Oct 2 candidate) and similarly on every date. With the outcome measured from +5 to +20 the association shrinks to 569/494, 575/513, 650/622, 736/726 against 652/540, 670/594, 661/540, 809/691 for the opposite conditioning, i.e. both reversal-leaning by about the same amount. Conditioning on +1 s events among seconds whose quote did not move in +1 s gives 290/330 vs 286/336 (adds toward vs away, Oct 2) and the same near-symmetry on Oct 3, 17, 18 and in the controls. The apparent "read the replenishment, then trade the reversal" relationship is the reversal itself, observed in the same second. This is the main caution for any feedback-based rule.

**Trading follows quote changes immediately.** The completed group after a midpoint-moving group carries a print in 0.35 to 0.38 of cases versus 0.03 to 0.04 after a flat group, with the next print 0.3 to 0.4 ms later; 0.36 to 0.39 even when the moving group itself had no print. At message speed this is either a sweep continuing or algorithmic reaction; it is not separable here.

**Large moves (price → book → price).** Half of the 2-tick jumps continue in the first 5 s and half reverse; the extreme is reached inside 5 s in most cases; 74 to 83% return fully within 600 s. The return is the clearest price-driven behavior in the packet, and it is the same mechanism as the 1-tick bounce at larger scale.

**Thin-level endings (mechanics of price through a level).** Retreats are produced by trades through the level in 0.56 to 0.63 of cases and by pulled quotes in 0.37 to 0.44; recoveries are replenishment in essentially all cases (fill-linked 1/0/0/0).

**Price → flow at family starts (Oct 18 only).** Flow at the start agrees with the prior 120 s price direction 0.62 to 0.67 of the time (flow follows price on this clock); the prior direction's continuation is 0.58 at 1 s and 0.47 at 60 to 300 s.

**Grid evidence.** Recent quote-change count (300 s) predicts adds and cancels at both touches over the next 5 s (0.16 to 0.22) and the next 20 s absolute move (0.13 to 0.19) on all four days. This is activity begetting activity and larger moves, not direction.

## 14. The dipole as an exit cue

The user's suggestion that the dipole suits exits better than entries was tested three ways in the first pass and re-read against the corrected convention in this rerun (tables/m12_*.json, scripts/m12_dipole_exit.py).

**Protective exit from the large-move fade.** A first pass suggested that a Native dipole recognized inside the 600 s window before any return marked the moves that never return (not returned 7/20, 9/16, 13/20, 15/29 with such a dipole versus 4/26, 1/42, 2/37, 0/35 without). That pass is survivorship: a move that returns in 7 s leaves no time for a dipole to be recognized inside it. At a fixed decision time of 60 s among positions still open, a dipole recognized in the jump direction preceded non-return in 1/4, 5/8, 2/2, 3/11 cases versus 7/17, 5/10, 10/19, 10/24 with no dipole: two dates lean one way, two the other, with counts too small to read. At 120 s: 4/8, 9/12, 7/11, 7/17 versus 7/13, 1/5, 6/7, 6/10. Not supported as a protective exit on these four days.

**Exit at recognition when the flow state is "opposite".** For a position held in the birth direction, exiting at an "opposite" recognition versus holding is neutral: adverse/favourable oriented moves at 300 s are 79/84, 86/100, 83/64, 94/105 (and 69/78, 88/70, 73/59, 101/80 at 60 s). The dipole is right that the run is spent (0.93 to 0.96 already ended), but the adverse movement it describes happened during the 50 s recognition lag, before the cue exists.

**Ccode seed states.** The seed state becomes known at t0+61 s on every event (median 61 s on all dates; none known by the 50 s horizon), so the apparent adverse move after O seeds measured from the 50 s horizon was not decision-time information. Measured from the moment the seed is known, the next 60 s is mixed across dates (O up/down 94/66, 88/73, 71/84, 85/104; S 65/66, 57/78, 59/81, 81/79).

**What does hold.** After a weakening onset of the dipole being ridden (fresh opposed lots, step, half or vanished flow), the subsequent move follows and opposes the dipole direction in near-equal counts on every date (continuous-prebirth outcome summaries; e.g. expiry-only weakening at 60 s follows/opposes 974/934 on Oct 2), with median absolute 60 s moves of 0.5 to 1.5 ticks. That is exposure without drive. The defensible exit use of the dipole is therefore "no remaining reason to hold" at the first fresh-opposed or vanished-flow onset, not "an adverse move is coming". It is recorded as observation-only overlay S6 in PAPER_STRATEGIES.md because it needs message-level flow that Tradovate does not supply, and because the onset outcomes were never measured conditional on an open position.

Correction from the rerun. The first pass's "after the first fresh opposed lot the next 60 s moves against" was the marking second's own tick; from the end of that second the next 60 s is symmetric on all four dates (section 5). The defensible exit use is therefore exactly the one the first pass's last paragraph gave: "no remaining reason to hold" at the exhaustion onset, not "an adverse move is coming".
