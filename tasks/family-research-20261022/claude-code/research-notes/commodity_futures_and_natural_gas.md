## KQ1. How a futures contract works, and the CME Henry Hub NG contract specifics

### Takeaway
A futures contract is a cleared, daily-marked-to-market obligation; the economically important mechanics for an intraday study are (a) margin and daily settlement, which make the 14:28-14:30 ET settlement print the reference for every open position's cash flow, (b) expiry three business days before the delivery month with physical delivery at Sabine's Henry Hub, and (c) the roll, which shifts liquidity between contract months and must be handled explicitly (Panama vs ratio back-adjustment) in any continuous series. Confirmed NG specs: 10,000 MMBtu, tick $0.001/MMBtu = $10, termination on the 3rd-last business day of the month before the contract month, standard options expire one business day before that.

### Cited Findings

**Contract specifications (NYMEX NG)**
- Contract unit: "10,000 MMBtu" — [CME contract specs](https://cmegroup.com/trading/energy/natural-gas/natural-gas_contractSpecs_futures.html)
- Minimum price fluctuation $0.001 per MMBtu, worth $10.00 per full-size NG contract — [NexusFi NG instrument page](https://nexusfi.com/a/instruments/natural-gas-ng-futures) (secondary; primary is the CME spec page above, verify)
- Termination: "Trading terminates on the 3rd last business day of the month prior to the contract month." — [CME contract specs](https://cmegroup.com/trading/energy/natural-gas/natural-gas_contractSpecs_futures.html); WTRG paraphrases the same rule as "Trading terminates three business days prior to the first calendar day of the delivery month." — [WTRG Economics](https://wtrg.com/daily/gasprice.html)
- Delivery point and mechanics: "Sabine Pipe Line Co.'s Henry Hub in Louisiana. Seller is responsible for the movement of the gas through the Hub; the buyer, from the Hub. The Hub fee will be paid by seller." Deliverable grade: gas "meeting the specifications set forth in the FERC-approved tariff of Sabine Pipe Line Company as then in effect at the time of delivery" — [CME contract specs](https://cmegroup.com/trading/energy/natural-gas/natural-gas_contractSpecs_futures.html) / [WTRG](https://wtrg.com/daily/gasprice.html)
- Physical delivery location is the Henry Hub interconnection in Erath, Louisiana — [CapTrader glossary](https://www.captrader.com/en/glossary/natural-gas-futures/) (secondary)
- Ratable delivery: the only explicit mention found was in the 2005 Hurricane Rita force-majeure notices ("Any remaining September and October natural gas obligations should commence and complete ratable delivery in the calendar month of October 2005") — [CME/NYMEX press release 2005-09-23](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2005/9/23/_nymex_reiteratesruleinlightofforcemajeuredeclarationbysabinepip.html); [2005-10-04 lift of force majeure](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2005/10/04/_nymex_lifts_forcemajeuredeclarationonnaturalgas.html). The general rule (delivery ratably over the calendar month) lives in NYMEX Rulebook Chapter 220, which was not fetchable — see [Chapter 440 PDF location pattern](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/rulebook/NYMEX/4/440.pdf) for the rulebook URL scheme.
- Related financially settled sibling: Henry Hub Penultimate Financial futures "Trading terminates on the 4th last business day of the month prior to the contract month" (i.e., one business day before NG) — [CME Penultimate Financial specs](https://cmegroup.com/markets/energy/natural-gas/natural-gas-penultimate-swap-financial.contractSpecs.html). Last-Day Financial options spec page: [CME](https://cmegroup.com/markets/energy/natural-gas/natural-gas-last-day.contractSpecs.options.html). Micro Henry Hub (MNG) specs: [CME](https://cmegroup.com/markets/energy/natural-gas/micro-henry-hub-natural-gas_contract_specifications.html).

**Options expiry**
- Standard monthly Henry Hub options: "Trading terminates on the 4th last business day of the month prior to the contract month (1 business day prior to the expiration of the underlying futures corresponding contract month)." — [CME, Getting started with Micro Henry Hub Natural Gas options (PDF)](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/energy/files/getting-started-with-micro-henry-hub-natural-gas-options.pdf). The same document describes a Friday weekly variant with a different termination offset (search summary said "two business days before futures expiry"; verify). Weekly options FAQ: [CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/faqs/faq-natural-gas-weekly-options.html); options suite fact card: [CME PDF](https://cmegroup.com/markets/energy/files/henry-hub-natural-gas-options-suite-fact-card.pdf).
- ICE's Henry-Hub-referenced options: in-the-money options "exercise automatically into the Underlying Futures Contract with a contract price equal to the Strike Price"; out-of-the-money "expire automatically"; pricing date is "One Business Day prior to the last scheduled trading day of the NYMEX Henry Hub Natural Gas Futures Contract for the Delivery Date" — [ICE product spec PDF](https://www.ice.com/api/productguide/spec/6590274/pdf)

**Trading hours and halts**
- Globex energy (CL, NG) trades roughly 23 hours, "from 06:00 p.m. until 05:00 p.m. Sunday to Friday" (ET) — [StoneX futures blog](https://futures.stonex.com/blog/money-never-sleeps-futures-trading-hours-run-23-5) (secondary). A schedule page lists the energy daily break as 4:00-5:00 PM CT Mon-Thu (= 5:00-6:00 PM ET) — [ptv-dev CME hours page](https://ptv-dev.pages.dev/blog/cme-trading-hours) (garbled text; verify on the CME spec page).
- "Regular trading hours" for NG futures begin at 9:00 a.m. ET; the pre-announcement drift studied by Kurov et al. "coincides with the beginning of the regular trading hours in the natural gas futures market at 9:00 a.m. ET" — [CLS Blue Sky Blog (Kurov, Sancetta, Strasser, Wolfe)](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2016/09/15/what-drives-informed-trading-before-public-releases)
- Energy futures use dynamic circuit breakers: "the range in which prices can move is reset continuously on a rolling 60-minute lookback window. If markets move +/- 10% in that time, a 2-minute halt is initiated" — [CME, Understanding Price Limits and Circuit Breakers](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/understanding-price-limits-and-circuit-breakers); contradicted in level by a March 2020 notice that NYMEX "is increasing the dynamic circuit breakers for the energy contracts from 7% to 15%" — [CME SER-8569 (PDF)](https://cmegroup.com/notices/ser/2020/03/SER-8569.pdf). Which level applied in October 2023 is unresolved here.
- NG has hit a DCB during the closing/settlement period (a two-minute halt on a January 27, reported as "highly unusual"; the CFTC said the moves were "consistent with recent supply and demand fluctuation") — [Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/natural-gas-rally-triggers-circuit-breaker-electronic-trade/). A Feb 25, 2026 Globex technical outage halted gas futures/options for ~35 minutes — [Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/us-natural-gas-and-metals-trading-disrupted-by-cme-technical-issues-2026225).

**Margin and marking to market**
- "Initial margin is the amount of funds required by CME Clearing to initiate a futures position. While CME Clearing sets the margin amount, your broker may be required to collect additional funds" — [CME Education, Margin: Know What's Needed](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/introduction-to-futures/margin-know-what-is-needed)
- "After the initial margin is met, a market participant is required to keep up maintenance margin. This is the amount of equity required to retain an open position"; typical maintenance is 80-90% of initial — [CME Education](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/introduction-to-futures/margin-know-what-is-needed)
- When equity falls below maintenance, "a call must be issued to bring the account up to initial margin" (unlike securities margin) — [CME Clearing, Performance Bonds/Margins](https://cmegroup.com/clearing/risk-management/performance-bonds-margins.html); current NYMEX margin tables: [CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/clearing/margins/initial-margin-requirements.html)
- Daily cash flow vs forwards: a futures position "is mark-to-market against the CME settlement curve" daily — [CME Education, Futures vs Forwards](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/introduction-to-base-metals/cash-flow-in-metals-futures-vs-forwards.html)

**Roll and continuous contracts**
- Panama (difference) back-adjustment "shifts all historical prices by adding or subtracting a constant at each roll point"; cumulative adjustments "can sometimes push historical prices into negative territory". Ratio adjustment "adjusts historical prices by multiplying them by the ratio of the new price to the old price", preserving percentage moves but not levels — [QuantVPS, Continuous futures explained](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/continuous-futures-contracts-explained); [NexusFi back-adjusted data](https://nexusfi.com/a/data/continuous-contracts-back-adjusted-data)
- Quantpedia uses first-of-month roll with backward ratio adjustment and stresses "there is not the one best algorithm" and that only adjusted prices should be used for backtests — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/continuous-futures-contracts-methodology-for-backtesting/)
- Practitioner warning: with ratio adjustment you cannot apply point value to adjusted prices; with difference adjustment percent changes become incorrect — [NexusFi thread](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=468915)

### Inferences
- For an MBO study of a single October 2023 contract month (e.g., NGX23, which terminated on the 3rd-last business day of October 2023), no back-adjustment is needed; it matters only if the study splices NGX23 into NGZ23 across the late-October roll. Spread VWAPs in the settlement window (KQ6) are the natural roll-gap reference.
- Because the margin call restores equity to *initial* rather than maintenance, forced liquidation pressure is lumpier than in securities markets; this is a plausible (unverified) contributor to settlement-period flow concentration.
- Options expiring one business day before futures means the penultimate trading day of the front month carries both option exercise/assignment flows and final futures positioning.

## KQ2. Who trades commodity futures and why; hedging pressure, theory of storage, term structure, normal backwardation debate

### Takeaway
Participants are formally classified by the CFTC into Producer/Merchant/Processor/User, Swap Dealer, Managed Money and Other Reportables (plus non-reportables), by predominant business purpose. Two competing frameworks explain risk premia and curve shape: hedging pressure (Keynes/Hicks normal backwardation, revived by Bessembinder 1992 and de Roon-Nijman-Veld 2000) and the theory of storage (Kaldor/Working: basis = interest + storage cost - convenience yield, with inventories the state variable). Gorton-Hayashi-Rouwenhorst find inventories explain the basis and risk premia and reject positions as predictors, so the literature is unsettled.

### Cited Findings

**Participant taxonomy (CFTC COT)**
- Producer/Merchant/Processor/User: "an entity that predominantly engages in the production, processing, packing or handling of a physical commodity and uses the futures markets to manage or hedge risks" — [CFTC Disaggregated Explanatory Notes](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/DisaggregatedExplanatoryNotes/index.htm)
- Swap Dealer: "an entity that deals primarily in swaps for a commodity and uses the futures markets to manage or hedge the risk associated with those swaps transactions" — [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/DisaggregatedExplanatoryNotes/index.htm)
- Managed Money: "a registered commodity trading advisor (CTA); a registered commodity pool operator (CPO); or an unregistered fund identified by CFTC"; Other Reportables is the residual — [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/DisaggregatedExplanatoryNotes/index.htm); [PDF version](https://WWW.cftc.gov/idc/groups/public/@commitmentsoftraders/documents/file/disaggregatedcotexplanatorynot.pdf)
- Classification is "based on the predominant business purpose self-reported by traders on the CFTC Form 40 and is subject to review by CFTC staff"; "staff classifies traders not their trading activity" — [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/DisaggregatedExplanatoryNotes/index.htm)
- Reports cover "each Tuesday's open interest for markets in which 20 or more traders hold positions equal to or above the reporting levels"; the legacy report splits "commercial" and "non-commercial"; the Disaggregated report began in 2009 — [CFTC COT descriptions](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm)

**Hedging pressure / normal backwardation**
- Keynes (1930) proposed normal backwardation: producers' forward selling pushes futures below expected spot; Kolb (1992) examined 29 contracts and concluded "normal backwardation is not normal" — [AnalystPrep summary](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/alternative-investments/theories-of-commodity-future-returns/) (secondary)
- de Roon, Nijman and Veld (2000, J. Finance 55(3)): across 20 futures markets in four groups, after controlling for systematic risk, a market's own hedging pressure and cross-hedging pressure from related markets both explain futures returns; hedging pressure also has explanatory power for the underlying asset's returns — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v55y2000i3p1437-1456.html); [Tilburg WP](https://ideas.repec.org/p/tiu/tiutis/3dfe2c9f-3194-4751-9b34-12828163330e.html)
- Bessembinder (1992, RFS): futures risk premia depend on systematic risk and on net hedger positioning — [secondary summary](https://qgtm-docs.pages.dev/research/gold_silver/paper_bessembinder_hedging_pressure) (unverified; the t-statistics on that page could not be checked against the paper)
- Rouwenhorst and Tang: variation in hedging pressure "embeds two components that predict futures returns with opposite signs" — [Rouwenhorst, A Tale of Two Premiums (PDF)](https://www7.uc.cl/economia/finance_uc/docs/conferences/11th/Rouwenhorst-Tale%20of%20two%20premiums.pdf)
- Fan, Fernandez-Perez, Fuertes, Miffre: short hedging is associated with higher expected futures returns across commodity, equity and currency futures, with no long-run effect in fixed income — [AUT working paper (PDF)](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/188161/AFP-Hedging-pressure-everywhere_Adrian-Fernandez-Perez_2018-DMC.pdf); related NBER paper [w19056](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19056.pdf)
- Horizon dependence: one presentation reports the hedging-pressure/return relation is negative at the longest frequencies (6-10 year planning cycles) and inconsistent at the shortest — [UC Denver JPMorgan Center session](https://business.ucdenver.edu/jpmorgancenter/content/session-2-presentation-2)

**Theory of storage**
- Kaldor (1939) introduced convenience yield; Working (1948, 1949) built the theory of storage on it and "proposed inventory levels as the variable underlying convenience yield" — [Nguyen, Carbonez, Sercu, KU Leuven WP (PDF)](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/bitstream/123456789/264567/1/AFI_1041.pdf); [Cristiano and Paesani chapter](https://art.torvergata.it/bitstream/2108/100254/1/Quad_Chap6_CRISTIANO-PAESANI.pdf)
- Fama and French (1987, J. Business 60(1):55-73): the theory of storage "explains the difference between contemporaneous futures and spot prices (the basis) in terms of interest changes, warehousing costs, and convenience yields" — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jnlbus/v60y1987i1p55-73.html)
- Gorton, Hayashi, Rouwenhorst (NBER w13249, 2007; Review of Finance 17(1), 2013): "Risk premiums vary across commodities and over time depending on the level of physical inventories, as predicted by the Theory of Storage"; "the convenience yield is a decreasing, nonlinear function of inventories"; "Low inventory levels for a commodity are associated with an inverted ('backwardated') term structure of futures prices, while high levels of inventories are associated with the opposite shape"; "we find no evidence that the positions of participants in futures markets predict risk premiums"; basis and prior returns "reflect the state of inventories and are informative about commodity futures risk premiums". WP sample: 31 commodities, 1969-2006; published: 1971-2010 — [NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w13249); [Review of Finance](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:17:y:2013:i:1:p:35-105); [Knowledge@Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-inventory-code-new-ways-investors-can-cash-in-on-volatile-commodities/)
- Theory of storage applied to NG: an ICE white paper argues storage operators' injection limits and "garage full" conditions can cause sharp intramonth moves, and large spring inventories can trigger a spot-price collapse — [ICE, Natural Gas Market Storage Dynamics and Alpha Generation](https://www.ice.com/white-paper/natural-gas-market-storage-dynamics-and-alpha-generation)
- Later NG-specific storage-theory work: "The theory of storage in the crude oil futures market, the role of financial conditions" (JFM 2020) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v40y2020i7p1160-1175.html) (listed, not read)

### Inferences
- For NG the two theories are not mutually exclusive on the seasonal cycle: the winter/summer contango-backwardation pattern is storage theory (inventories high in October → contango into winter is *expected*; backwardation emerges when inventories are low relative to the seasonal norm), while the sign of managed-money net positioning is a hedging-pressure variable that GHR say does not forecast premia.
- The "term structure tells you inventories" reading (GHR) implies the October 2023 curve, with storage ~5% above the five-year average (KQ3), should have been in seasonal contango with a compressed winter premium, which matches the record-low widowmaker premium reported for 2023 (KQ3).

### Gaps
- COT release clock (Friday 3:30 p.m. ET for Tuesday positions) was NOT confirmed by any retrieved source; the CFTC overview page should state it — [CFTC COT index](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm).
- No source retrieved on index investors / GSCI-BCOM roll windows (5th-9th business day) or on market makers/HFT share in NG; search budget exhausted.
- Hirshleifer's hedging-pressure theory and Hicks' version of normal backwardation: no primary source retrieved.
- Bessembinder 1992 relies on an unverified secondary page.

## KQ3. Natural gas specifics: storage report, consensus, weather, seasonality, LNG/power/production, widowmaker, volatility, Henry Hub and basis, October 2023 context

### Takeaway
The EIA Weekly Natural Gas Storage Report (Thursday 10:30 a.m. ET, data as of 9 a.m. the prior Friday, sampled via Form EIA-912) is the single scheduled fundamental shock in NG; consensus (Bloomberg/Reuters analyst surveys) is highly accurate (median forecast anticipates >99% of variation) so prices react to the residual. Weather enters via model runs on a fixed 00/06/12/18 UTC cadence. October 2023 sat at the end of the injection season with storage ~5% above the five-year average, Henry Hub spot near $2.90-$3.18, record production (113.1 Bcf/d marketed) and record LNG feedgas, which compressed the winter premium to a record low.

### Cited Findings

**EIA Weekly Natural Gas Storage Report: clock, methodology**
- Release: "Thursday at 10:30 a.m." ET, with changes posted on the release schedule page — [EIA WNGSR methodology](https://ir.eia.gov/ngs/methodology.html); the 2024 evaluation restates "the following Thursday at 10:30 a.m. eastern time (ET), unless the release schedule is affected by a holiday or other pre-approved reasons" — [EIA WNGSR performance evaluation 2024 (PDF)](https://ir.eia.gov/ngs/wngsrevaluation_2024.pdf)
- Reference point: data "as of 9 a.m. Friday" — [EIA methodology](https://ir.eia.gov/ngs/methodology.html) (so the Thursday release describes the week ending the previous Friday; a 6-day lag)
- Holiday weeks shift the release to a Wednesday at 12:00 p.m. or a Friday at 10:30 a.m. — [EIA schedule page (test mirror)](https://ir.eia.gov/ngs_test/schedule.html); live: [EIA information releases](https://ir.eia.gov/)
- Sampling: "EIA draws a sample for the EIA-912 from the census of storage operators that report on Form EIA-191, the monthly storage report"; estimation uses "a 12-month moving average of working gas volumes (based on the latest published EIA-191 monthly data), and the latest EIA-912 weekly data" — [EIA methodology](https://ir.eia.gov/ngs/methodology.html) (page last updated September 2018)
- The WNGSR is EIA's only Principal Federal Economic Indicator — [EIA Today in Energy](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=3370)
- History: before EIA, the American Gas Association published the weekly survey; EIA took over with the report after May 6, 2002 — [Linn and Zhu 2004, JFM 24(3)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v24y2004i3p283-313.html)
- Weekly report archive (numbers by week): [Oil & Gas 360, week ending Oct 6 2023](https://www.oilandgas360.com/weekly-gas-storage-10-6/), [Oct 13](https://www.oilandgas360.com/weekly-gas-storage-10-13/), [Oct 20](https://www.oilandgas360.com/weekly-gas-storage-10-20/) (listed; contents not read)

**How consensus forms and how accurate it is**
- Gay, Simkins and Turac studied "the weekly storage predictions of the 34 firms and those in the Bloomberg weekly survey of analysts' predictions between 1997 and 2004"; analysts assist "significantly in the price discovery"; "significant cross-sectional differences in analyst forecasting ability"; changes are "more difficult to forecast during withdrawal periods than during injection periods"; average error 13.2 Bcf — [NGI](https://www.naturalgasintel.com/study-finds-average-13-2-bcf-error-in-analysts-weekly-storage-predictions-2/)
- Ederington, Lin, Linn and Yang (Energy Journal 40(5), 2019): the median Bloomberg forecast is "highly accurate (and basically unbiased) anticipating over 99% of the variation in natural gas storage figures", and "the market promptly incorporates these analyst forecasts into oil and gas prices prior to the EIA announcements" — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/enejou/v40y2019i5p121-142.html); [IAEE exec summary](https://www.iaee.org/ej/ejexec/EJ40-5_Ederington_ExecSum.pdf)
- Recent illustration of the surprise convention (reported build vs survey): a 101 Bcf build vs 96 Bcf forecast — [VT Markets](https://www.vtmarketsmy.com/live-updates/eia-storage-build-undershoots-demand-growth-keeping-us-natural-gas-prices-under-pressure/) (vendor commentary)

**Weather models and degree days**
- GFS "is run four times daily at 00z, 06z, 12z and 18z" — [NOAA NCEI GFS 0.5 degree metadata](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ncdc:C00634/html); ECMWF also "4 per day (0z, 6z, 12z, 18z)" — [MetDesk models help](https://help.metdesk.com/info/models)
- ECMWF dissemination schedule (provisional, verify): 00 UTC control forecast delivered 08:30 UTC, 12 UTC run 20:30 UTC — [ECMWF dissemination schedule](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/spaces/DAC/pages/272310483/Dissemination+schedule)
- Trader usage (2013, dated): "the most closely watched run by NatGas and Power traders is '12z run' which starts around noon for Operational and closer to 1pm for Ensemble model"; "between 11AM and 1PM, model run drastically moves markets" — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/general/13/07/3786945/a-tool-to-monitor-midday-weather-model-run-frame-by-frame)
- Anecdotal: NG futures can "spike 15 cents in two minutes" on a model update — [AlphaEx Capital guide](https://www.alphaexcapital.com/commodities/energy-commodities/natural-gas-trading/weather-models-gas-trading) (vendor); HDD as the key demand metric — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/heating-degree-days-natural-gas-how-hdd-data-moves-price) (vendor)
- GFS run timing notification (2021, GFS v16): [NWS SCN21-21 (archived PDF)](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20230320172543/https://www.weather.gov/media/notification/pdf2/scn21-21model_timing_gfs_v16aaa.pdf) (listed; not read)

**Seasonality**
- "Analysts refer to the storage injection season as running from April 1 through October 31 of each year, but these dates are not strict cutoff points"; net injections occurred in November in each of 11 years studied and in March in 6 of 11 — [EIA Today in Energy](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=18211)
- "inventories decrease from November through March, the withdrawal season, when natural gas consumption is relatively high"; the South Central region occasionally shows summer withdrawals — [EIA Today in Energy (2024)](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67524)

**The widowmaker (March/April, H/J) spread**
- The H/J spread "is the gap between the March and April contracts"; March covers the end of winter, April the start of injection, so it "reflects the winter risk premium" — [tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/closing-the-gap-futures-edition/episodes/natural-gas-the-widow-maker-spread-10-21-2016); [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4317025-natural-gas-widow-maker-spread)
- In 2020 the spread "turned negative for the first time" as winter approached — [NGI](https://www.naturalgasintel.com/natural-gas-set-to-open-lower-after-brief-collapse-of-widow-maker-spread/); [Investing.com](https://uk.investing.com/analysis/natural-gas-widowmaker-spread-turns-negative-in-new-risk-to-bulls-200450964)
- Record spread of $2.13/MMBtu in May 2022 amid LNG demand; the 2023 price collapse "cut 2023 widow-maker premium to record low" — [Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/u-s-natgas-price-collapse-cut-2023-widow-maker-premium-to-record-low/)
- The Amaranth loss on this spread is reported variously (search summary relayed "$8 billion"/"2017" from one source and "over $6 billion" from another; the commonly cited collapse date is September 2006 — the retrieved sources conflict and the 2017 date is almost certainly an error in the relayed summary; verify) — [tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/closing-the-gap-futures-edition/episodes/natural-gas-the-widow-maker-spread-10-21-2016); [OilPrice](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Widowmaker-Natural-Gas-Trade-Collapses-On-Mild-Winter-Forecast.amp.html)

**Why NG is more volatile than crude**

## KQ4. Price formation across horizons: intraday (releases, order flow, model runs), daily, seasonal/structural; fundamentals vs flow

### Takeaway
The storage-announcement literature is the best-identified evidence on how a fundamental enters the NG tape: price moves inversely to the storage surprise on the release day only, futures lead spot, the response roughly doubled after 2005, OLS understates it (by ~4x for NG per Halova-Kurov-Kucher), volatility stays elevated for ~30 minutes, and there is a pre-announcement drift beginning ~90 minutes before (from the 9:00 a.m. ET open) whose cause is contested (informed trading vs an asymmetric puzzle concentrated on bearish-surprise days).

### Cited Findings
- Linn and Zhu (2004, JFM 24(3):283-313): the weekly storage report "is associated with price volatility at the time of its release and extends up to 30 minutes following the announcement", for NYMEX nearby NG futures around both the AGA report and the EIA report after May 6, 2002 — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v24y2004i3p283-313.html); [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/natural-gas-prices-and-the-gas-storage-report-public-news-and-volatility-in-energy-futures-markets-linn-scott/10006818980); author's 2012 IMF seminar slides: [linn.pdf](https://0-www-imf-org.library.svsu.edu/external/np/seminars/eng/2012/commodity/pdf/linn.pdf), [linn2.pdf](https://0-www-imf-org.library.svsu.edu/external/np/seminars/eng/2012/commodity/pdf/linn2.pdf)
- Chiou-Wei, Linn and Zhu (2014, JIMF 42:156-173): "an inverse empirical relation between changes in futures prices and surprises in the change in natural gas in storage" that "is not driven by the absolute size of the surprise"; "Post 2005, corresponding to a period of significant increases in the production of natural gas in the United States, the response of prices to storage surprises was larger in absolute value"; "No evidence is found of economically meaningful reactions to the surprise other than on the date the storage news is released"; sample Aug 30, 2002 - Aug 18, 2011; no effect of analyst-forecast dispersion — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jimfin:v:42:y:2014:i:c:p:156-173); [EconBiz](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/the-response-natural-gas-futures-and-spot-prices-storage-change-surprises-fundamental-information-and-the-effect-escalating-physical-gas-production/10011048440)
- Halova, Kurov and Kucher (2014, JFM 34(10):911-933, "Noisy Inventory Announcements and Energy Prices"): OLS estimates of the price impact of inventory surprises "are substantially biased"; identification-through-censoring estimates are "about twice as large for petroleum commodities and about four times as large for natural gas"; uses intraday futures data — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jfutmk:v:34:y:2014:i:10:p:911-933); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v34y2014i10p911-933.html); full text (host blocked): [Skidmore PDF](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/NoisyInventoryAnnouncementsAndEnergyPrices.pdf)
- Kurov, Sancetta, Strasser and Wolfe (JFM 2018, 38(9):1079-1096; summarized 2016): "The pre-announcement returns and order flow in natural gas futures are significantly correlated with inventory surprises based on the consensus analyst forecast"; "The pre-announcement drift begins about 90 minutes before the announcement, which coincides with the beginning of the regular trading hours in the natural gas futures market at 9:00 a.m. ET. The sharp increase in trading activity around that time makes it easier for informed traders to hide their trading"; candidate sources: leaks, or "informal surveys of natural gas storage operators"; "informed trading due to superior processing of public information is a plausible explanation" — [CLS Blue Sky Blog](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2016/09/15/what-drives-informed-trading-before-public-releases); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/wly/jfutmk/v38y2018i9p1079-1096.html)
- Prokopczuk, Wese Simen and Wichmann (Energy Journal 42(2), 2021, "The Natural Gas Announcement Day Puzzle"): "The pre-announcement return is entirely generated on days when storage levels exceed analysts' expectations casting doubt on explanations based on informed trading" — [IAEE exec summary (PDF)](https://iaee.org/ej/ejexec/ej42-2-Wichmann-exsum.pdf); SSRN author page: [Wichmann](https://papers.ssrn.com/author=3575861)
- Chebbi (2019, Int. J. Global Energy Issues 42(1/2)): with daily data, "strong evidence of a negative effect of inventory surprises on energy futures returns on the announcement day, while the effect after that day becomes insignificant" — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijgeni/v42y2019i1-2p1-20.html)
- Ederington, Lin, Linn, Yang (2019): the market "promptly incorporates these analyst forecasts into oil and gas prices prior to the EIA announcements" — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/enejou/v40y2019i5p121-142.html)
- Weather-model runs as intraday clock events (00/06/12/18 UTC; 12z most watched, ~11:00-13:00 ET) — [NOAA NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ncdc:C00634/html); [Benzinga 2013](https://www.benzinga.com/general/13/07/3786945/a-tool-to-monitor-midday-weather-model-run-frame-by-frame)
- Structural/seasonal drivers 2023: record production, LNG feedgas, consumption (see KQ3) — [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=57261); storage surplus path through October 2023 — [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=61044)
- Other related work found but not read: Reading University NG paper (storage announcements) — [CentAUR PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/90003/1/NG_paper_final_round.pdf); Mu (IAEE best paper, weather and NG volatility) — [IAEE PDF](https://iaee.org/en/students/best_papers/Xiaoyi_Mu2.pdf); Skidmore "Bidirectional causality in oil and gas markets" — [PDF](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/BidirectionalCausalityInOilAndGasMarkets.pdf)

### Inferences
- Horizon mapping supported by the literature: the storage surprise is a *release-day* effect with no measurable drift afterward (Chiou-Wei et al.; Chebbi), so at the daily-and-longer horizon fundamentals are impounded via the consensus (Ederington et al.) rather than the print; at the minutes horizon the print plus its noise component dominates (Halova et al.'s censoring correction implies the raw tape under-reacts to the *measured* surprise because the surprise variable is noisy, i.e., attenuation bias).
- The 90-minute pre-release drift plus its concentration on bearish-surprise days (Prokopczuk et al.) means a flow-based nowcast at 9:00-10:30 ET on Thursdays is a plausible lead on the print direction, but the asymmetry is a warning that any directional signal should be evaluated per surprise-sign cell, not pooled.
- The post-2005 increase in price sensitivity to storage surprises coincides with the shale production ramp; with 2023 production at a record, the October 2023 sample sits in the high-sensitivity regime.
- Weather-model timings are fixed in UTC, so DST transitions (US clocks changed Nov 5, 2023) shift the model-run clock relative to ET by an hour at the end of the October 2023 sample; event-study alignment should be done in UTC.

### Gaps
- Coefficient magnitudes (cents per Bcf of surprise) were not retrieved for any of the papers; the working-paper PDFs (ou.edu, skidmore.edu) were blocked.
- No literature retrieved on order-flow price impact in NG at the MBO level, on intraday volume/liquidity profiles (9:00 open, 10:30 release, 14:30 settle), or on market-maker/HFT behaviour in NG; search budget exhausted.
- No retrieved source on "fundamentals vs flow at which horizon" beyond the announcement studies; the question remains open in these notes.

## KQ5. Spread trades, options hedging, and feedback into the outright tape

### Takeaway
The clearest mechanical link found is that NYMEX settles deferred NG months off the VWAP of calendar-spread trades in the settlement window, so spread order flow literally defines the settled curve; the widowmaker and other seasonal spreads carry the winter risk premium. On options feedback (dealer gamma, strike pinning) only trading-education sources were found and none offered NG-specific evidence; treat as hypothesis.

### Cited Findings
- Deferred-month settlements are derived from "the VWAP of front-to-second-month spread trades in the same window, anchored to the front-month settlement", requiring at least 100 contracts of spread volume; spread volume is "divided by the number of months between the legs when it is combined with outright volume" — [NYMEX submission to CFTC, 2019 (PDF)](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule091718nymexdcm005.pdf); [NYMEX Submission 19-198 (PDF)](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule080819nymexdcm001.pdf)
- Seasonal spread (H/J) as the vehicle for winter risk — [tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/closing-the-gap-futures-edition/episodes/natural-gas-the-widow-maker-spread-10-21-2016); [Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/u-s-natgas-price-collapse-cut-2023-widow-maker-premium-to-record-low/)
- Options pinning claims: pinning "often happens near strike prices with high open interest"; dealers short gamma hedge mean-revertingly ("buy when price falls, sell when price rises"); the effect is "strongest" when OI is concentrated and expiry is hours away — [Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/how-dealers-use-futures-to-hedge-options-and-why-it-moves-the-market); negative-gamma regimes amplify moves — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/max-pain-options-expiration-futures); crude oil hedging flows claimed to move price near expiry/low liquidity — [OilPrice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Crude-Oil/Why-Oil-Reacts-Violently-at-Random-Levels.amp.html). All are vendor/education sources without methodology.
- Vendor gamma-exposure dashboards exist for NG futures options and UNG but are outputs, not evidence — [OptionsAnalysisSuite NGN6](https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/futures/ngn6/gamma-exposure)
- NG options expire one business day before the futures (KQ1), so option-related hedging flows land on the penultimate futures trading day — [CME Micro options PDF](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/energy/files/getting-started-with-micro-henry-hub-natural-gas-options.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because deferred settles are spread-derived, a trader needing a specific deferred settle can influence it via spread prints in the two-minute window at lower notional than via outrights; this is a structural reason to expect spread activity to cluster at 14:28-14:30 ET.
- Any gamma/pinning effect in NG should be tested per expiry-week cell around the options expiry (4th-last business day) and the futures expiry (3rd-last), never pooled with ordinary days.

### Gaps
- CME Globex implied-spread functionality (implied-in/implied-out pricing between outright and spread books) was not retrieved; it bears directly on how spread orders appear in MBO outright data.
- No academic evidence on option-expiry pinning or dealer hedging in energy futures was found in this session.
- Inter-commodity spreads (NG vs power spark spread, NG vs CL) not researched.

## KQ6. Settlement: how CME computes the NG daily settlement; the window; why price behaviour differs near settlement

### Takeaway
NG settles off CME Globex trades in the 14:28:00-14:30:00 ET window: the front month at the VWAP of outright trades (rounded to the tick), the next months via spread VWAPs anchored to the front, with bid/ask fallbacks at 14:30. Because the settlement is the mark for all variation margin and for settlement-referenced derivatives, it is the one price each day that many participants are benchmarked to, which concentrates flow in the window.

### Cited Findings
- "CME Group staff settle the first six NG contract months from trades on CME Globex in that two-minute window" (14:28-14:30 ET) — [NYMEX/CFTC filing 2019 (PDF)](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule091718nymexdcm005.pdf); [CME NYMEX Natural Gas settlement procedure page](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/MgCmGw)
- Front month: "the VWAP of outright trades in the window, rounded to the nearest tick" — [NYMEX/CFTC filing 2015 (PDF)](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule060815nymexdcm001.pdf)
- Deferred months: VWAP of spread trades anchored to the front settle; 100-contract minimum; spread volume scaled by months between legs when combined with outrights — [NYMEX/CFTC 2019 (PDF)](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule091718nymexdcm005.pdf); [Submission 19-198 (PDF)](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule080819nymexdcm001.pdf)
- Fallback: "If there is no VWAP, the last trade is checked against the 14:30 ET bid/ask, and the price moves to the nearest bid or ask if the last trade falls outside it" — [CME procedure page](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/_ZlDGw)
- E-mini natural gas (QG) settles from the regular NG settlement rounded to its tick — [CME procedure page](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/MgCmGw); Natural Gas settlement wiki page: [CME](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/lZlDGw)
- Historical note: FERC's older NYMEX-settlement analyses (2003-2011) describe a 14:00-14:30 window, so the two-minute window is a later refinement — [FERC 2011 NYMEX settlement analysis (archived PDF)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120927231802/http://www.ferc.gov/market-oversight/reports-analyses/special-analyses/2011-nymex-set.pdf); [FERC 2003 (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20080916005858/http://ferc.gov/market-oversight/reports-analyses/overview/2003-nymex-set.pdf)
- Daily variation margin is computed against the settlement — [CME Education, Futures vs Forwards](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/introduction-to-base-metals/cash-flow-in-metals-futures-vs-forwards.html)
- A dynamic circuit breaker halt has occurred inside the NG closing period — [Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/natural-gas-rally-triggers-circuit-breaker-electronic-trade/)

### Inferences
- The settlement is a VWAP, not a last print, so participants targeting it must trade *through* the two minutes, which mechanically raises volume and makes the window's price a liquidity-weighted average rather than a marginal price; price behaviour immediately before 14:28 (positioning) and after 14:30 (unwinding/overnight) is structurally different from the window itself. This is consistent with the project's existing practice of excluding the daily-settle window from backtests.
- Because the first six months are settled from window trades and later months from spread relationships, the settlement window is also when the *curve* (not just the front) is fixed, which draws spread and strip traders into the same two minutes.
- For the project's data: the internal repo encodes the EIA release at 14:30 UTC (10:30 ET) and Kalshi's KXNATGASD settle at 21:00 UTC; the NYMEX 14:28-14:30 ET window corresponds to 18:28-18:30 UTC during EDT (October 2023 through Nov 4) and 19:28-19:30 UTC after the Nov 5, 2023 clock change (inference from the ET times above; verify).
