## 1. What quants are looking for, how edges decay, the factor zoo and multiple-testing corrections

### Takeaway
Quants hunt four kinds of edge (risk premia, behavioural anomalies, liquidity provision/microstructure, information speed), and the published evidence says most "discoveries" are inflated: anomaly returns fall ~26% out-of-sample and ~58% post-publication (McLean-Pontiff), a published strategy's Sharpe roughly halves after publication (CFM), and the cumulative hurdle for a new factor is t > 3.0, not 2.0 (Harvey-Liu-Zhu). The practical response is to deflate performance for the number of trials (deflated Sharpe ratio, PBO, minimum backtest length) and to pool many weak, roughly independent signals rather than hunt for one strong one.

### Cited Findings
- McLean and Pontiff (Journal of Finance 2016) re-examine 97 published return predictors: long-short returns are ~26% lower out-of-sample (an upper bound on data-mining/statistical bias) and ~58% lower post-publication; combining the two gives a lower bound on the publication (investor-learning) effect of ~32%. Declines are larger for predictors with higher in-sample returns and for portfolios concentrated in high-idiosyncratic-risk, low-liquidity stocks (arbitrage costs). — [McLean & Pontiff PDF (gwern mirror)](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [Counterpoint Funds copy](https://counterpointfunds.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/PredictabilityMcleanPontiff.pdf)
- A follow-up of 241 anomalies across 39 markets finds the United States is the only country with a reliable post-publication decline in long/short returns; none of 38 international markets shows one, so the 58% figure may be US-specific. — [Jacobs & Mueller seminar paper](https://www.wiwi.hu-berlin.de/en/Professorships/bwl/finance/seminars/papers-fin-acc-res-seminars/17s/jacobs.pdf)
- Harvey, Liu and Zhu ("...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", NBER w20592 / RFS 2016) catalogue 313 published papers plus 63 working papers covering 316 factors (mid-1960s to 2012) and apply a multiple-testing framework that allows for correlation among tests and missing data; they conclude a newly discovered factor should clear t > 3.0 (p < 0.0027), that the usual t > 2.0 "does not make any economic or statistical sense" given cumulative data mining, and that "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false". — [NBER w20592](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:20592); [SSRN 2513152](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152)
- A secondary summary of an earlier version puts one-third to one-half of 296 published factors as likely false positives; it also notes factor studies use gross returns and that factors with strong theory may deserve leniency. (secondary) — [CXO Advisory, Taming the factor zoo](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/taming-the-factor-zoo/)
- Bailey and Lopez de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (JPM 2014): DSR corrects for two sources of performance inflation, selection bias under multiple testing and non-normally distributed returns, "to separate legitimate empirical findings from statistical flukes". — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)
- Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (J. Computational Finance 2017): combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV) estimates the probability that a backtest is overfit by checking whether in-sample rankings of configurations hold out-of-sample; the CRAN `pbo` package implements it, reporting PBO, performance degradation and stochastic dominance. A secondary note observes CSCV's block reshuffling weakens strict temporal ordering, so PBO complements rather than replaces walk-forward tests. — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [pbo vignette](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/pbo/vignettes/pbo.html); [LuxAlgo PBO note (secondary)](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/probability-of-backtest-overfitting/)
- Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism" (Notices of the AMS 2014): the more configurations tried, the more likely the best backtest is overfit; analysts rarely report the number of trials; they derive a Minimum Backtest Length (MinBTL) as a function of the number of trials N, approximately 2 ln(N) / E[max_N]^2 years, and stress it is a necessary, not sufficient, condition. — [WMU ScholarWorks record](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/); [CARMA PDF](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf); [Stats 207 lecture notes with the bound](https://cs.runi.ac.il/~kipnis/notes/STATS_207_L18.pdf)
- CFM-linked study of 60 published equity strategies: the Sharpe ratio declines by about one half after publication; small-sample bias is a major contributor to overfitting; sensitivity of a strategy to removing a small subset of stocks predicts its post-publication decay. — [arXiv 2105.01380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)
- CFM ("Lucking Up"): the t-statistic of a strategy's P&L equals its Sharpe ratio times the square root of N, where N is the number of years the strategy is active. — [CFM 187 Lucking Up](https://www.cfm.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/187-Lucking-Up.pdf)
- CFM slide deck (Rej/Adam, Columbia): in the absence of shocks the portfolio Sharpe grows with the square root of the pool size N of (independent) signals, the formal basis of the "we need many weak signals" stance. — [RejAdam slides](https://cfe.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/RejAdam_slides.pdf)
- Bouchaud (CFM) on the two main families: trend following has a long-term Sharpe around 0.8-1 while mean reversion has about 0.2-0.3, which is why he weights trend more heavily. — [Risk.net, Fischer Black was right. Somewhat](https://www.risk.net/our-take/6022966/fischer-black-was-right-somewhat)
- Harvey, Arnott, Kalesnik and Linnainmaa, "Alice's Adventures in Factorland": the first of three factor-investing blunders is exaggerated expectations from data mining and overfit historical samples (the others: crowding and tail risk). — [Duke Fuqua](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/node/4257)

### Inferences
- The multiple-testing literature implies the effective hurdle for any new intraday signal is set by how many signal/parameter/cell combinations were examined, not by the single best p-value. A project that scans many "cells" (moneyness x side x lag-class x release) is running a large N of trials and should track that N explicitly and deflate accordingly (DSR / MinBTL logic).
- CFM's t = SR x sqrt(years) is the cleanest reminder that with few independent days, no intraday Sharpe computed over ticks is "significant" in the statistical sense; the unit of independence is the day (or the event), not the tick.
- Both McLean-Pontiff and the CFM half-life study say ~half of a documented edge is the realistic post-discovery expectation; a research program should budget for that shrinkage before promoting any backtest.

## 2. Commodity-futures signal families and the evidence (trend, carry/term structure, hedging pressure, basis-momentum, fundamentals), plus intraday energy patterns

### Takeaway
The robust, repeatedly-replicated commodity signals are time-series momentum (positive in every one of 58 contracts 1985-2009; positive every decade since 1880 across 67 markets), carry/term structure (within-asset-class Sharpe 0.5-0.9; commodity carry ~0.5-0.64), hedging-pressure/COT sorts (Sharpe above long-only benchmarks, though Gorton-Hayashi-Rouwenhorst find no predictive power in positions), and basis-momentum (JF 2019, outperforms basis and momentum, not explained by storage theory). For energy intraday, the best-documented facts are that the weekly EIA storage release produces a volatility spike persisting ~30 minutes, that more than half of natural gas futures' annual return accrues on EIA announcement days, and that the return-surprise relation is strongly negative.

### Cited Findings
- Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, "Time Series Momentum" (JFE 104(2), 2012): 58 liquid futures (equity indices, currencies, commodities, bonds), monthly 1985-2009; the past 12-month excess return positively predicts the next 1-12 months, with partial reversal over longer horizons (consistent with under-reaction then delayed over-reaction); 12-month TSMOM profits are positive for every one of the 58 contracts; using weekly CFTC Commitments of Traders data, speculators profit from TSMOM at the expense of hedgers. Returns are gross. — [EconPapers JFE record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250); [SSRN 2089463](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463); [AQR dataset page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data)
- Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing" (JPM 2017): equal-weighted 1-, 3- and 12-month TSMOM across 67 markets (29 commodities, 11 equity indices, 15 bond markets, plus currencies) from 1880; positive average returns in every decade; performed well in 8 of the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns; secondary summaries give an average net Sharpe of ~0.4 after 2%/20% fees and drawdowns of up to ~25%. (Sharpe/drawdown figures secondary.) — [Paper PDF (TrendFollowing.com mirror)](https://www.TrendFollowing.com/whitepaper/Century_Evidence_Trend_Following.pdf); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/trend-following-with-intrinsic-momentum-over-the-very-long-run); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/time-series-momentum-aka-trend-following-the-historical-evidence/)
- Lemperiere et al. (CFM), "Two centuries of trend following": trend across four asset classes over two centuries; the search extract did not expose the exact t-statistics (listed in Gaps). — [ar5iv 1404.3274](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1404.3274)
- Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt, "Carry" (JFE 127(2), 2018): expected return = carry + expected price change; a carry trade within each asset class earns an annualised Sharpe of 0.5-0.9; the diversified cross-asset carry portfolio Sharpe is 1.1 in the Nov-2013 draft (1.4 in the 2012 draft); slides list commodity carry Sharpe 0.50 (current carry) and 0.64 (1-12 month carry). — [EconPapers JFE record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:127:y:2018:i:2:p:197-225); [NBER w19325](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19325); [Koijen slides (FMG)](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/Ralph-Koijen-slides.pdf); [accepted manuscript (CBS)](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/files/57294842/lasse_heje_pedersen_et_al_carry_acceptedmanuscript.pdf)
- Basu and Miffre, "Capturing the Risk Premium of Commodity Futures: The Role of Hedging Pressure" (JBF 37(7), 2013): long-short portfolios from single sorts on hedgers' or speculators' open interest and double sorts on both earn positive, significant premia with Sharpe ratios that systematically exceed long-only commodity benchmarks; the premia rise when commodity volatility rises; hedging pressure predicts the cross-section differently from past returns and term-structure slope; the portfolios are better equity diversifiers. — [EconPapers JBF record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:37:y:2013:i:7:p:2652-2664); [EDHEC summary](https://climateinstitute.edhec.edu/publications/capturing-risk-premium-commodity-futures-role-hedging-pressure)
- Fan, Fernandez-Perez, Fuertes and Miffre, "Speculative Pressure" (J. Futures Markets 2020) extend the Basu-Miffre approach to currency, equity-index and fixed-income futures. — [City Research Online PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/23283/1/FINAL_JFM__Accepted_27Nov2019_SSRN.pdf)
- Gorton, Hayashi and Rouwenhorst, "The Fundamentals of Commodity Futures Returns" (Review of Finance 17(1), 2013; NBER w13249): 31 commodity futures with physical inventory data; the convenience yield is a decreasing, non-linear function of inventories; the futures basis, prior futures returns and spot returns are informative about risk premia because they proxy inventory state; excess returns to momentum and backwardation strategies stem in part from selecting low-inventory commodities; they find NO evidence that futures-market participants' positions predict risk premia (contradicting the hedging-pressure reading of Basu-Miffre). A secondary summary reports the momentum hedge portfolio positive in 58% of months with 13.4% average annual return. — [NBER w13249](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13249); [EconPapers RoF record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:17:y:2013:i:1:p:35-105); [Macrosynergy summary (secondary)](https://macrosynergy.com/?p=233)

A blog on an early (2015) draft quotes Sharpe 0.93 (spot) and 0.99 (spreading). - Prokopczuk, Wese Simen and Wichmann, "The Natural Gas Announcement Day Puzzle" (Energy Journal 42(2); earlier working paper): more than 50% of the annual return of natural gas futures is generated on weekly EIA storage announcement days; the intraday analysis uses a two-hour window around the release; there is a strong, significant negative relationship between NG futures returns and the announcement surprise. — [Reading working paper PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/90003/1/NG_paper_final_round.pdf); [Energy Journal executive summary](https://iaee.org/ej/ejexec/ej42-2-Wichmann-exsum.pdf)

- A Padova working paper studies crude oil, natural gas and ethanol intraday data including overnight returns, volume and realised volatility (details not exposed by the search extract). — [EI2016-02 PDF](https://research.unipd.it/retrieve/e14fb26b-c58e-3de1-e053-1705fe0ac030/EI2016-02.pdf)

### Inferences
- The signal families that survived decades of replication are all slow (monthly holding periods, 1-12 month lookbacks). None of the canonical commodity "factors" speaks to minute-scale behaviour; the intraday evidence that exists for energy is about scheduled announcements (EIA storage, WPSR) and open/close volatility, i.e. event-time structure, not continuous-time signals.
- The hedging-pressure dispute (Basu-Miffre find it priced; Gorton-Hayashi-Rouwenhorst find positions do not predict premia) is a live example of the "works on {X}, not {Y}" pattern: the result depends on sorting method, sample and conditioning, so COT-based signals should be treated as conditional, not universal.
- Prokopczuk et al.'s ">50% of annual return on announcement days" plus Linn-Zhu's 30-minute elevated-volatility window together say that for NG the realistic intraday research unit is the announcement event and its first half hour; the rest of the week is comparatively low-information for directional work.

## 3. Research methodology: hypothesis-first vs data mining, validation protocols, dependence, event studies, censoring, calibration

### Takeaway
The standard protocol is: state the hypothesis and the number of trials up front; validate with walk-forward or purged/embargoed cross-validation (and CPCV for many backtest paths); correct standard errors for overlapping windows and autocorrelation (Newey-West, block/stationary bootstrap); use event-study design for scheduled releases; and evaluate probabilistic forecasts with proper scoring rules and reliability diagrams, not hit rates. Sullivan-Timmermann-White's Reality Check is the canonical demonstration that the best of a large universe of technical rules shows no genuine outperformance once data snooping is accounted for.

### Cited Findings
- Sullivan, Timmermann and White, "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap" (Journal of Finance 54(5), 1999, pp. 1647-1691): applies White's Reality Check bootstrap to the full universe of simple technical rules (expanding Brock-Lakonishok-LeBaron's 26 rules) on 100 years of daily DJIA data; the working-paper abstract reports no evidence that the best technical rule outperforms once data-snooping effects across the whole universe are accounted for in the out-of-sample period. — [EconPapers JF record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:54:y:1999:i:5:p:1647-1691); [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119144); [Sheppard course PDF](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Sullivan_Timmermann_White.pdf)
- Purged k-fold cross-validation (Lopez de Prado, 2017/2018): training observations whose label horizon overlaps the test window are purged, and an embargo (temporal buffer after the test set) is added to stop serial-correlation leakage; combinatorial purged CV (CPCV) builds C(N,K) train/test splits that can be assembled into multiple backtest paths. Primary reference: Advances in Financial Machine Learning (Wiley 2018), ch. 7 (purging/embargo) and ch. 12 (CPCV). — [Wikipedia: Purged cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation); [purgedcv package docs](https://pypi.org/project/purgedcv/0.0.6/)
- Overlapping labels: a label that resolves over several periods overlaps its neighbours, so a plain shuffled split lets future information leak into training. — [purgedcv docs](https://pypi.org/project/purgedcv/0.0.6/)
- Politis and Romano, "The Stationary Bootstrap" (JASA 1994): resample blocks of random, geometrically distributed length so the resampled series is stationary; the 1994 work reports estimates "much less variable" than the moving-block bootstrap; block-length choice has a dramatic effect on finite-sample properties, so it should not be used without guidance on block size (Politis-White automatic selection). — [UCY library record](https://gnosis.library.ucy.ac.cy/handle/7/57533); [Politis-White block length paper](https://uzh.ch/cmsssl/econ/dam/jcr:ffffffff-935a-b0d6-0000-0000064f3c74/jnp2006.pdf); [Statistical Science review](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-18/issue-2/The-Impact-of-Bootstrap-Methods-on-Time-Series-Analysis/10.1214/ss/1063994977.pdf)
- Newey-West HAC standard errors: bandwidth chosen by automatic asymptotic criteria; prewhitening is a standard refinement (relevant for overlapping-horizon return regressions). — [Stata US26 Baum slides](https://www.stata.com/meeting/us26/slides/US26_Baum.pdf); [Fed FEDS 1996-45](https://www.federalreserve.gov/PUBS/FEDS/1996/199645/199645pap.pdf)
- Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz, "A Backtesting Protocol in the Era of Machine Learning" (JFDS 2019): a seven-point protocol (research motivation/hypothesis first; multiple-testing awareness; data cleaning and outliers; cross-validation discipline; model dynamics/nonstationarity; complexity; research culture); it asks researchers whether they understand that most tests will fail. — [SSRN 3275654 PDF (mirror)](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/SSRN-id3275654.pdf); [Hudson & Thames summary](https://hudsonthames.org/?p=10523)
- Luo et al. (Deutsche Bank 2014), "Seven Sins of Quantitative Investing": survivorship bias (#1), look-ahead bias, storytelling, data mining/snooping, signal decay/turnover/transaction costs, outliers, asymmetric payoff/shorting; Palomar's textbook notes these are "basic backtesting errors that most journal publications make routinely". — [Columbia copy of the DB paper](https://columbia.edu/~nyc2107/papers/seven_sins_of_quantitative_investing.pdf); [Palomar, Portfolio Optimization book ch. 8.2](https://bookdown.org/palomar/portfoliooptimizationbook/8.2-seven-sins.html)
- Ernie Chan on pitfalls: look-ahead bias ("using tomorrow's price as today's signal"); best cure is running the same code in backtest and live so a look-ahead crashes live; survivorship bias matters most for long-only mean-reversion and less for momentum; data-snooping bias grows with number of rules/parameters, so keep models simple and grounded in economic rationale. — [Benzinga, Backtesting Pitfalls](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/13/05/3582742/backtesting-pitfalls); [notes on Algorithmic Trading book](https://josephchenhk.github.io/ernest-p-chan-winning-strategies-and-their-rationale-1)
- Vontobel AM: look-ahead bias is the most common backtesting mistake; remedy is to timestamp each input with its first public release and use it only after. — [Vontobel, Backtesting gone wrong](https://am.vontobel.com/insights/backtesting-gone-wrong)
- Brier score is a strictly proper scoring rule (expected score minimised by reporting true belief); Murphy decomposition = reliability (calibration) + resolution (discrimination) + uncertainty (base-rate variance); two forecasters can share a Brier score with very different components; the Brier Skill Score compares against a climatology baseline because the raw score depends on base rate; a low Brier score does not by itself prove calibration. — [MetricGate, Brier score explained](https://metricgate.com/blogs/brier-score-explained/); [calibration methodology page](https://calibrationledger-com.pages.dev/methodology/)
- Reliability diagrams plot observed frequency vs predicted probability (calibrated = diagonal); binned diagrams are unstable, and the CORP approach (isotonic regression / pool-adjacent-violators) yields stable, optimally binned diagrams; the "triptych" pairs reliability, ROC and Murphy diagrams, and the area under a Murphy curve equals the mean Brier score. — [Dimitriadis, Gneiting, Jordan (KIT)](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000130510/106591216); [Triptych paper arXiv 2301.10803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10803)
- Prediction-market forecast evaluation asks whether market-implied probabilities were accurate and calibrated over time. — [FinFeed glossary](https://www.finfeedapi.com/learn/glossary/forecast-evaluation)

### Inferences
- For one-tick order-book data with few independent days, the right cross-validation unit is the day (or the release event): purge any training sample whose label window touches a test day, embargo the following session, and bootstrap at the block/day level (stationary bootstrap with block length of at least a session) rather than resampling ticks. Tick-level resampling will massively overstate significance because intraday observations are strongly dependent.
- A flat/no-change outcome at a given horizon is a censoring/ties problem; the sources here do not give a finance-specific protocol, but proper-scoring-rule logic suggests scoring a probabilistic three-way forecast (up/flat/down) with a multiclass Brier/log score rather than discarding flats, and reporting the reliability component separately. (Inference; see Gaps.)
- CPCV's many backtest paths are especially valuable when the number of days is small, because they give a distribution of out-of-sample Sharpe rather than one path; but CPCV's reshuffling must still respect event-day boundaries.

### Gaps
- Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay (1997) event-study chapter: no retrieved source; the standard abnormal-return / cumulative-abnormal-return design with estimation window and event window is well known but not cited here.
- No source found on how to treat flat/no-change outcomes or right-censoring of intraday horizon labels (e.g., triple-barrier labelling in AFML ch. 3) — AFML book text not accessible.
- Could not retrieve Newey-West guidance specific to overlapping event-window returns (Hansen-Hodrick / Britten-Jones et al.).
- Stationarity/regime-change testing protocols (structural break tests, rolling re-estimation) not covered by retrieved sources.

## 4. Machine learning in finance: what worked (Gu-Kelly-Xiu), where it fails, order-book deep learning and its critiques

### Takeaway
Gu, Kelly and Xiu showed ML improves cross-sectional stock return prediction, but the absolute predictability is tiny (monthly out-of-sample R^2 of roughly 0.26-0.40%), the economic gain shows up only in portfolio Sharpe (~1.35 for the neural-net long-short decile), and independent replication has struggled. In order-book deep learning, Sirignano-Cont found a "universal" order-flow-to-price relation that transfers to unseen stocks and DeepLOB reports F1 ~83% on the 10-day FI-2010 benchmark, but the 2023 cross-dataset benchmark (Prata et al.) found most LOB models overfit FI-2010 and degrade sharply on unseen LOBSTER data.

### Cited Findings
- Gu, Kelly and Xiu, "Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning" (RFS 2020; NBER w25398): report a high out-of-sample predictive R^2 relative to prior literature, robust across ML specifications; a value-weighted long-short decile strategy from stock-level neural-net forecasts earns an annualised out-of-sample Sharpe of 1.35. A summary gives the headline monthly OOS R^2 of ~0.26% after penalisation/dimension reduction (a plain OLS with 900+ predictors overfits badly); another paper quotes the best NN monthly OOS R^2 as 0.40%. — [NBER w25398](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf); [Mirova summary (secondary)](https://www.research-center.mirova.com/en/research-library/Empirical-Asset-Pricing-via-Machine-Learning); [Cambridge CRERC WP quoting 0.40%](https://www.landecon.cam.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-08/CRERC_2024-02%20WP.pdf)
- An Erasmus University thesis attempting replication "cannot replicate the performance of Gu, Kelly, and Xiu": their neural networks produced negative OOS R^2 and only XGBoost gave positive, much smaller R^2 (student thesis; suggestive only). — [Erasmus thesis](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/59944/513098rh-thesis.pdf)
- A characteristics-model paper notes that with a finite testing period the computed OOS R^2 can be higher or lower than its population value, so monthly R^2 of 0.2-0.4% sits inside sampling noise. — [AEA 2021 paper](https://topcat.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/FdAdyHdN)
- Sirignano and Cont, "Universal features of price formation in financial markets: perspectives from deep learning" (Quantitative Finance 19(9), 2019): deep learning on a large high-frequency dataset of US equity quotes and trades; a nonparametric search for a universal, stationary relation between order flow and price moves; out-of-sample direction-of-move accuracy is remarkably stable across stocks and sectors; a model trained on all stocks outperforms asset-specific linear and nonlinear models; results hold for stocks not in the training sample; including long order-flow history improves forecasts (path dependence). — [arXiv 1803.06917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06917v1); [EconPapers QF record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:19:y:2019:i:9:p:1449-1459); [Imperial Spiral record](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/handle/10044/1/62927)
- A later AAAI paper summarises the Sirignano-Cont LSTM as predicting next mid-price movement with around 70% accuracy across all 500 stocks. (Secondary.) — [AAAI paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16133/15940)
- Zhang, Zohren and Roberts, "DeepLOB" (IEEE TSP 2019): CNN+Inception+LSTM on raw LOB; the authors note the FI-2010 benchmark is only 10 consecutive days of down-sampled, pre-normalised data; on a year of LSE data they report k=20 accuracy of 70.17% on seen stocks and 68.62% on unseen stocks; a secondary summary gives FI-2010 F1 of 83.40% at k=10 (setup 2) vs 77.63% for C(TABL). (FI-2010 F1 secondary.) — [arXiv 1808.03668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.03668); [Emergent Mind DeepLOB topic (secondary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/deeplob)
- Prata et al., "LOB-based deep learning models for stock price trend prediction: a benchmark study" (Artificial Intelligence Review 2024; arXiv 2308.01915): benchmarks the major LOB models; best models achieve solid F1 on FI-2010 but show poor generalisability, with performance dropping significantly on unseen LOBSTER data; "most of them overfit the FI-2010 dataset". — [arXiv 2308.01915](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01915.pdf); [Springer AIR article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-024-10715-4)
- A secondary note: DeepLOB's utility is contingent on microstructure, strongest on large-tick, liquid, information-rich instruments and diminished on small-tick assets with sparse order flow. — [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/deeplob)
- Hudson & Thames (summarising Arnott-Harvey-Markowitz and Lopez de Prado): experimental-design pitfalls of ML in finance include leakage across overlapping labels and failure to account for the number of trials. — [Hudson & Thames](https://hudsonthames.org/?p=10523)

### Inferences
- The LOB literature's own numbers (70% next-tick direction accuracy; F1 drop from ~83% to much lower on a different venue) say that microstructure predictability is real at the next-event horizon but is venue-, tick-size- and regime-specific; a model fit on one contract's book in one season should be assumed not to transfer until shown otherwise.
- Sirignano-Cont's "path dependence over many past observations" and their universality result argue for pooling the training set across days and instruments to fight the few-independent-days problem, while testing strictly on held-out days; this is the one place where "pooling" is defensible (for estimation), without pooling the evaluation.
- GKX's lesson transfers: expect R^2 that look like noise and judge signals by decision-relevant outcomes (ranked portfolios / per-event calls), while remembering that a 1.35 Sharpe from an R^2 of 0.3% is exactly the kind of result that a replication failed to reproduce.

### Gaps
- Ntakaris et al. (2018) FI-2010 benchmark paper itself not retrieved; its baseline accuracies are not cited here.
- Direct methodological critiques of the GKX R^2 metric (benchmark choice, zero-forecast baseline) not found.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of LOB deep learning on futures (CME MBP-10) order books was found; everything retrieved is equities.

## 5. Execution and market impact: Almgren-Chriss, the square-root law, self-impact estimation, capacity, and the gross-vs-tradeable gap

### Takeaway
Impact is the bridge between a gross signal and a tradeable edge. The empirical square-root law (impact ~ Y * sigma * sqrt(Q/V), exponent ~1/2) holds across asset classes, periods, execution styles and venues, is linear only for very small orders, and in the square-root regime is independent of execution time; Almgren-Chriss (linear impact) is the institutional scheduling workhorse but misstates large-order impact, and Almgren et al. (2005) estimated an empirical exponent of ~3/5 from bank data. Capacity follows from these: as size grows, impact grows concavely but the edge does not, so capacity is where impact cost equals the expected gross edge.

### Cited Findings
- Bouchaud: the square-root law exponent stays near 1/2 even as underlying power-law exponents differ across stocks; some models predict a concave impact tied to tail exponents, but the exponent is anchored at ~0.5. — [Bouchaud Substack, The Square-Root Law of Market Impact](https://bouchaud.substack.com/p/the-square-root-law-of-market-impact)
- The law holds "to a large degree independent of details such as asset class, time period, execution style and market venues". — [Market impact with multi-timescale liquidity (ar5iv 1710.03734)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.03734)
- Donier, Bonart, Mastromatteo and Bouchaud: a dynamical theory of latent liquidity predicts a V-shaped average supply/demand profile around the current price; the anomalously small local liquidity breaks linear response and explains the square-root law. — [Donier et al. PDF (UvA)](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/a.khedher/winterschool/18DonierEtAl.pdf); [Toth et al. arXiv 1105.1694](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1105.1694)
- Crossover: impact is linear in Q for small Q at fixed T and crosses over to square-root for large Q; in the square-root regime impact is predicted to be independent of execution time T. — [Crossover from linear to square-root impact (arXiv 1811.05230)](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1811.05230)
- Almgren-Chriss is "the workhorse of institutional execution" but its linear-impact assumption does not capture square-root scaling, so a plain calibration misstates impact for large orders. — [Delphic Alpha reference guide on impact models](https://delphicalpha.substack.com/p/reference-guides-market-impact-models)
- Almgren, Thum, Hauptmann and Li, "Equity Market Impact" (Risk 2005): large bank dataset; impact split into a permanent (information) component and a temporary component; impact scales with block size to roughly the 3/5 power, with dependence on trade duration, daily volume, volatility and shares outstanding; results feed directly into optimal trade scheduling and pre-/post-trade cost estimation. — [Preprint (UPenn)](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/costestim.pdf); [Risk.net article](https://www.risk.net/derivatives/structured-products/1500270/equity-market-impact)
- Han, Wu and Cheng (Journal of Risk) estimate an impact exponent of ~0.7 for the Chinese market, higher than the 3/5 figure. — [Journal of Risk](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-risk/5861701/equity-market-impact-modeling-an-empirical-analysis-for-the-chinese-market)
- Man AHL's "The Need for Speed": using its own trading-cost models, risk-adjusted returns after costs are materially lower for faster trend speeds over the long term, though fast models retain defensive value; an older AHL statement puts its fast end at one-to-two-day holding and notes its alpha degrades more slowly than HFT alpha; Man's $21bn quant fund "sees no need for high frequency trading". — [HedgeNordic on Need for Speed](https://hedgenordic.com/2023/03/the-need-for-speed-in-trend-following-strategies); [Risk.net on Man AHL and HFT](https://www.risk.net/node/1730110)
- Renaissance anecdote: with a 50.75% hit rate and equal wins/losses, a worked example yields ~$1.50 per trade before costs and turns negative at ~$2 average cost, illustrating that a small hit-rate edge survives only with very low execution cost; Zuckerman estimates Medallion leverage at ~12.5x on average, sometimes 20x. — [Elite Economy, The Medallion Fund](https://eliteeconomy.substack.com/p/the-medallion-fund); [Institutional Investor on Cornell's analysis](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)

### Inferences
- "How quants estimate their own impact": the retrieved theory implies the procedure practitioners use: regress realised price drift during and after their own metaorders on sqrt(Q/V) scaled by volatility to get their own Y, then separate temporary (decaying) from permanent impact; no source in this session describes the procedure explicitly (see Gaps).
- For a project that deliberately excludes trade cost from research outputs, the concept still matters as a sanity bound: an intraday edge measured in cents on NG must eventually be compared to Y*sigma*sqrt(Q/V) at the intended size, and the square-root form says the edge-to-impact ratio deteriorates as size^(1/2), which defines capacity.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved on typical values of the constant Y (commonly reported ~0.5-1) or on explicit capacity formulas; the Bouchaud post could not be fetched.
- No source on futures-specific impact calibration (CME CL/NG) or on how the square-root law behaves for thin overnight books.

## 6. Practitioner viewpoints: how systematic funds generate ideas, what survives, how they treat explanations, why they keep weak signals

### Takeaway
Practitioner accounts converge on a few habits: a formal multi-step signal pipeline (Renaissance: find the anomaly, confirm it is statistically significant/consistent/non-random, then look for a reasonable explanation but keep it even if the explanation is missing); most academic strategies fail to replicate when tested in-house; weak rules are kept and down-weighted rather than discarded (Carver); rule pools are kept small, simple and low-correlation; and portfolio Sharpe is built by pooling many roughly independent low-Sharpe signals (CFM's sqrt(N) logic).

### Cited Findings
- Renaissance (per Zuckerman): staff settled on a three-step process to discover statistically significant trading signals: find anomalous patterns in historical prices, confirm they are statistically significant, consistent and non-random, then check whether a reasonable explanation exists; it was not immediately obvious why some signals worked, but signals with p-values under 0.01 were still kept; by 1997 over half the signals discovered were ones they could not completely understand; staff read many academic papers but when they tested a paper's strategy, most failed to live up to the published results. — [Novel Investor book notes](https://novelinvestor.com/notes/the-man-who-solved-the-market-by-gregory-zuckerman/); [A Wealth of Common Sense summary](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2019/11/non-intuitive-lessons-from-the-man-who-solved-the-market/)
- Robert Mercer reportedly said Medallion was right 50.75% of the time across millions of trades (second-hand, via Zuckerman; no audited confirmation). — [Elite Economy summary](https://eliteeconomy.substack.com/p/the-medallion-fund); [Quartr summary](https://quartr.com/insights/company-research/explore-renaissance-technologies-and-the-medallion-fund)
- Robert Carver (Systematic Trading / Leveraged Trading, as summarised): he will not drop rules based on historic performance alone, saving that data for forecast weights; reducing the pool of rules considered is crucial unless you have many years of data; rules must be simple, objective, explicable and intuitive because complicated rules are likely overfit; he excludes variations with >95% correlation and dislikes rules that trade too fast or too slow; rules are combined into a weighted-average forecast rather than pass/fail selected; diversification across instruments beats diversification across rules (10 instruments SR 0.45 vs 10 rules SR 0.34 in his backtest). — [CXO, A few notes on Systematic Trading](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/a-few-notes-on-systematic-trading); [7 Circles, Systematic Trading 3](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24066); [7 Circles, Leveraged Trading 5](https://the7circles.uk/?p=27890); [Better System Trader ep. 26](https://bettersystemtrader.libsyn.com/026-robert-carver)
- Man AHL, "Trend Following and Drawdowns: Is This Time Different?" (June 2025): tests crowding against history; shows a fast trend system (two-day-weighted MA crossover, costed, ~100 markets) with declining efficacy over time alongside systematic pairs trading, yet concludes trends persist and the recent weakness looks cyclical with no sign of crowding. — [Man Institute](https://www.man.com/insights/is-this-time-different)
- Winton organises academic staff into research teams that peer-review and test new strategies. — [Wikipedia: Winton Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winton_Group)
- Bouchaud/CFM: trend-following Sharpe 0.8-1 vs mean-reversion 0.2-0.3 long term; portfolio Sharpe scales as sqrt(N) over independent signals; t-stat = SR*sqrt(years). — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/our-take/6022966/fischer-black-was-right-somewhat); [RejAdam slides](https://cfe.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/RejAdam_slides.pdf); [CFM Lucking Up](https://www.cfm.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/187-Lucking-Up.pdf)
- Rob Arnott: "I have never seen so many billions of dollars invested into strategies based on a backtest" and the assumption that a backtest gauges future returns is not safe. — [IPE interview](https://www.ipe.com/strategically-speaking-research-affiliates/10030902.article)
- Ernie Chan: prefer simple, linear strategies as an antidote to overfitting/data-snooping; his book treats the pitfalls as "almost as valuable as the strategy descriptions" because they make live results diverge from backtests. — [Book excerpt PDF](https://download.e-bookshelf.de/download/0000/8068/90/L-G-0000806890-0002328615.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Renaissance pipeline is an explicit hypothesis-light / statistics-first process with explanation as a tie-breaker, the opposite of the academic "theory first" prescription; the two are reconciled by extreme trade counts (millions) that make p < 0.01 meaningful. With few independent days that luxury is absent, so the explanation step has to carry more weight.
- Carver's "weight, don't drop" rule and CFM's sqrt(N) logic both argue against discarding a signal that works on only some cells: keep it on those cells with a low weight, and let the portfolio of cells do the work; this matches the "works on {X}, not {Y}" reporting discipline.

### Gaps
- No practitioner source found giving a concrete fraction of researched ideas that survive to production (the search for AQR/Man/Winton/Two Sigma attrition numbers returned nothing citable).
- Exact quotes attributed to Simons ("we don't need to understand why") could not be verified against the book text.
- No CFM/Bouchaud talk transcript on overcrowding was retrievable; the sqrt(N) statement comes from a slide snippet.

## 7. What quants struggle with in natural gas and energy futures: regimes (shale, LNG), weather jumps, the storage report, thin overnight books, volatility seasonality

### Takeaway
The energy-specific difficulties documented in the sources are: (a) the weekly EIA storage report concentrates return and volatility into a scheduled window (>50% of annual NG return on announcement days; volatility elevated ~30 minutes after release) so most "signal" is event-time; (b) seasonality and the shoulder-vs-winter spread are being reshaped by shale flexibility and LNG exports, with analysts disagreeing on whether this is a regime change (CME/Aegis: yes, more or shifted seasonal volatility; Wood Mackenzie: weather still dominates, no paradigm shift); (c) realised volatility itself cycles (Henry Hub front-month annualised vol ~81% in Q4 2024 falling to ~69% by mid-2025), so any fixed-threshold signal is non-stationary by construction. Rigorous evidence on thin overnight books in NG was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Linn and Zhu (2004): storage-report release produces considerable volatility with elevated volatility persisting up to 30 minutes; heavy volatility at the open and close of the NYMEX NG session; sample spans the AGA survey era and the first EIA months. — [Linn & Zhu PDF](https://ou.edu/content/dam/price/Management/Energy%20Institute/docs/Linn%20and%20Zhu%20JFM%202004.pdf)
- Prokopczuk, Wese Simen and Wichmann: >50% of NG futures' annual return is earned on EIA announcement days; strong negative return-surprise relation; two-hour intraday window analysed. — [Reading WP](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/90003/1/NG_paper_final_round.pdf); [Energy Journal exec summary](https://iaee.org/ej/ejexec/ej42-2-Wichmann-exsum.pdf)
- Market example (May 2026): EIA reported a 101 Bcf build above forecasts; prices retreated from intraday highs but stayed positive (anecdote, not evidence). — [NGI](https://www.naturalgasintel.com/?p=107079)
- Henry Hub front-month annualised volatility fell from ~81% in Q4 2024 to ~69% by mid-2025 (Bloomberg data relayed by EIA); the same piece warns LNG capacity growth and data-centre power demand could start the next volatility cycle. — [Investing.com analysis](https://www.investing.com/analysis/natural-gas-markets-finally-chill-out-after-years-of-turmoil-200664317)
- CME Group: shale's ability to ramp production quickly has partly decoupled supply from seasonal constraints, making NG seasonality less predictable as year-round drivers grow. — [CME, Is Nat Gas Seasonality Changing?](https://www.cmegroup.com/news/2024/is-nat-gas-seasonality-changing.html)
- Aegis Hedging: expanding US LNG export capacity, outage risk and seasonal utilisation could make summer and winter prices behave differently and fluctuate more; the 2022-2023 Freeport LNG outage shows a single facility can move the market. — [Aegis Hedging](https://www.aegis-hedging.com/insights/higher-seasonal-volatility-is-possible-as-reliance-on-lng-exports-increases)
- Wood Mackenzie analyst (via Oil & Gas Investor): weather can still outweigh export effects, so no paradigm shift in gas-price seasonality; a related piece reports the shoulder-month vs winter pricing gap nearly disappeared in one year with LNG/Mexico exports acting as steadier baseload. — [Hart Energy](https://hartenergy.com/exclusives/oil-and-gas-investor-natgas-shoulder-months-canceled-195550); [UGCenter copy](https://www.ugcenter.com/exclusives/oil-and-gas-investor-natgas-shoulder-months-canceled-195550)
- A 2016 LUT master's thesis finds higher NG price volatility in the later part of its sample and a growing summer effect alongside winter seasonality, linked partly to shale extraction (dated; supporting context only). — [LUT thesis](https://lutpub.lut.fi/bitstream/10024/129963/2/Masters%27%20Thesis_Polikarpova%20Maria_2016.pdf)
- Public power / utility commentary and EIA note the weekly storage report as the dominant scheduled catalyst for NG; the WPSR (Wednesdays) plays the same role for crude. — [APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/node/28024); [StoneX trading hours](https://futures.stonex.com/blog/commodity-trading-hours-timing-is-everything)

### Inferences
- Because the EIA storage window carries most of the return and a 30-minute volatility tail, an intraday NG research design that treats each Thursday release as the independent unit has on the order of 50 observations per year per contract; that is the true sample size for any announcement-conditioned claim, and the multiple-testing hurdles of section 1 apply to it.
- The vol-regime drift (81% to 69% within a year) means any signal defined in absolute dollar or tick thresholds is implicitly regime-dependent; thresholds should be normalised by trailing realised volatility or evaluated per volatility regime (the per-cell principle).
- The shale/LNG seasonality debate is unresolved in the sources; a research program should expect the seasonal baseline (and the sign of shoulder-month behaviour) to move across years and must not pool across pre- and post-LNG-export eras without a break test.

- No evidence retrieved on NG overnight (Globex) book thinness, spreads or depth; the claims about ETH vs RTH liquidity in the results are equity-index anecdotes.
- No peer-reviewed work found quantifying weather-forecast-revision jumps in NG futures at intraday resolution.
