# Alternative and Unconventional Frameworks for How Markets Work and Why Prices Move

Scope note for the report writer: these notes separate FRAMEWORK (what the theory claims) from EVIDENCE (what has actually been measured), and for every framework state (a) the core claim, (b) what it explains that standard theory does not, (c) a concrete prediction for a commodity-futures limit order book (LOB), and (d) the strongest evidence against it. LOB predictions are my inferences from the framework unless a source is attached; they are listed under "Inferences" and labelled UNTESTED where no study was found. Access constraints: direct fetches of arxiv.org and nber.org were blocked in this session and the web-search budget was exhausted, so several abstracts are cited through search-result summaries, RePEc/EconPapers listings or mirror pages rather than the full paper. Where a specific number could not be confirmed from a primary page, it is flagged.

## 1. Econophysics and statistical mechanics of order books

### Takeaway
The econophysics programme has produced two genuinely predictive, out-of-sample-tested results (the square-root impact law and the zero-intelligence prediction of spread/volatility from order-flow rates) and two heavily contested ones (log-periodic bubble prediction and the Hawkes "criticality" reading). The latent-order-book picture is the most useful framework for a commodity LOB because it makes explicit quantitative predictions about what happens when a large flow stops or reverses.

### Cited Findings

**Zero-intelligence (ZI) order-book model (Farmer, Patelli, Zovko 2005, PNAS)**
- Core claim and evidence: agents place orders at random (Poisson arrivals of market orders, limit orders uniformly placed, random cancellations); yet the model, parameterized only by measurable order-flow rates, explains about 96% of the cross-stock variance in the bid-ask spread and about 76% of the variance in the price diffusion rate on London Stock Exchange data. — [arXiv cond-mat/0309233](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0309233); [PNAS via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC548562)
- What it explains that standard theory does not: spread and volatility as mechanical consequences of order-flow rates rather than of information asymmetry (Glosten-Milgrom/Kyle); "the predictive power of zero intelligence" is the title claim. — [INET Oxford listing](https://www.inet.ox.ac.uk/publications/the-predictive-power-of-zero-intelligence-in-financial-markets)
- Strongest evidence against / limits: Mike and Farmer (2007) report the ZI model captures how the spread moves with order flow but does not predict the absolute size of these effects nor the correct distributional shapes; its volatility predictions are weaker; the uniform-placement assumption is unrealistic, and the authors call the model "inadequate in many respects". Their replacement ("empirical behavioral model") adds long-memory order signs, a Student-t placement distribution and a conditional cancellation process. — [Mike and Farmer 2007 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.org/html/0709.0159)

**Latent order book / reaction-diffusion (Donier, Bonart, Mastromatteo, Bouchaud 2015, Quant. Finance 15(7):1109-1121)**
- Core claim: the relevant object for impact is not the visible book (dominated by HFT market makers) but a hidden "latent" book of low-frequency intentions; near the current price its dynamics are universal and described by reaction-diffusion, with a single emergent parameter, the market liquidity L. The model reproduces the square-root impact law and predicts non-trivial price trajectories when a metaorder is interrupted or reversed; prices decompose into a transient "mechanical" part and a permanent "informational" part; the framework is free of price manipulation and prices can be made diffusive. — [arXiv 1412.0141](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.0141v3); [Quant. Finance listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:15:y:2015:i:7:p:1109-1121)
- Extension: Benzaquen and Bouchaud add finite deposition/cancellation rates, which modifies the square-root law and the impact decay at finite memory. — [Benzaquen & Bouchaud (UvA mirror)](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/a.khedher/winterschool/18BenzaquenBouchaud.pdf); [multi-timescale liquidity (ar5iv 1710.03734)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.03734)
- Precursor theory and futures evidence: Toth et al. (2011, Phys. Rev. X) argue that latent liquidity is vanishingly thin near the current price (V-shaped average supply/demand profile), which produces a diverging impact of small orders, i.e. the square-root impact law. Measured on CFM proprietary futures metaorders June 2007-Dec 2010: exponent roughly 0.5 for small-tick contracts and roughly 0.6 for large-tick contracts, over participation rates from a few 1e-4 to a few percent; they stress the anomalously HIGH impact of SMALL orders, not saturation of large ones. — [Toth et al. 2011 (arXiv 1105.1694)](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1105.1694v3)
- Impact decay after a metaorder ends: Bucci, Benzaquen, Lillo, Bouchaud (ANcerno, >8 million institutional metaorders) find impact reverts to about two-thirds of peak by end of the same day and converges at ~50 days to a non-zero plateau of about 1/2 of end-of-day impact (about 1/3 of peak); multiday autocorrelation of metaorder signs requires deconvolution. — [Bucci et al. (arXiv 1901.05332)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05332). Contradicted in part by Brokmann, Serie, Kockelkoren, Bouchaud (CFM), who argue the mechanical component decays all the way to zero and apparent persistence comes from predictability of order flow. — [Brokmann et al. (arXiv 1407.3390)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.3390)
- Long memory of order flow: Bouchaud, Farmer, Lillo (2008 review) state that because revealed liquidity is extremely low, large orders are executed incrementally over periods as long as months, so order-flow sign is a highly persistent long-memory process; efficiency then requires asymmetric liquidity adjustment and a slow decay of impact; agents are "at best weakly informed" and much of the information in prices comes from supply/demand itself. — [Bouchaud, Farmer, Lillo 2008 (arXiv 0809.0822)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.0822v1.pdf)
- Bouchaud's broader stance (the "unfortunate complexity" essay, follow-up to Nature 455, 1181, 2008) and the textbook Trades, Quotes and Prices (Bouchaud, Bonart, Donier, Gatheral, CUP 2018), which organises price formation around the LOB mechanics and an "ecology of financial markets" chapter. — [EconBiz record of the essay](https://econbiz.de/Record/the-unfortunate-complexity-of-the-economy-bouchaud-jean-philippe/10005083571); [Cambridge chapter listing](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/trades-quotes-and-prices/ecology-of-financial-markets/A1F040BF17951A236FD5D3465042D2FD)

**Minority games (Challet and Zhang 1997/1998; Santa Fe lineage)**
- Core claim: N inductive agents choose between two sides; the minority wins; a phase transition exists in the control parameter (memory/N), with an analytical volatility expression in one regime. — [Challet & Zhang 1998 (arXiv cond-mat/9805084)](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9805084)
- Evidence: Challet, Chessa, Marsili, Zhang (2001) report fat tails and volatility clustering arise AT the phase-transition point, the crossover to random-walk prices is a finite-size effect, and markets are argued to sit near criticality where they are "marginally efficient". — [CCMZ 2001, Quant. Finance 1(1):168-176](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v1y2001i1p168-176.html)
- Challet and Galla (2004): a vanishing return autocorrelation does not imply efficiency; minority games are used to show predictability can hide in higher-order statistics. — [arXiv cond-mat/0404264](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0404264)
- Johnson, Hart, Hui, Zheng extend with heterogeneous horizons/confidence thresholds and produce large fluctuations in participation "volume" and price. — [arXiv cond-mat/9910072](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9910072)
- Strongest evidence against: no search result reported an out-of-sample fit of a minority game to real returns with predictive success; the literature is theory and stylized-fact reproduction. (See Gaps.)

**Self-organized criticality (SOC) and power laws**
- Evidence for: Bartolozzi, Leinweber, Thomas report power laws in avalanche size, duration and laminar times in index data after wavelet filtering, but concede a "near-SOC" state or a chaotic/time-dependent driver can produce the same signatures, that the laminar-time memory is inconsistent with classical SOC, and that some tuning is needed for power laws to appear (contradicting the "self-organized" part). — [arXiv physics/0601171](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0601171); [arXiv cond-mat/0405257](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0405257)
- Evidence against the inference "power law => criticality": Clauset, Shalizi, Newman (SIAM Review 2009) show least-squares power-law fits are systematically biased, recommend MLE + KS goodness-of-fit + likelihood-ratio tests against log-normal/stretched-exponential/truncated power laws, and find the data are "often compatible with other distributions as well". A secondary review of the paper reports the log-normal was competitive with the power law in almost all two dozen datasets. — [Clauset et al. (arXiv 0706.1062)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1062); [Shalizi blog summary](https://www.bactra.org/weblog/491.html); [Broido & Clauset 2019, log-normal fits networks as well or better](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6399239)

**Log-periodic power law singularity (LPPLS) bubble prediction (Sornette)**
- Core claim: bubbles are positive-feedback regimes with faster-than-exponential price growth decorated by log-periodic oscillations converging to a critical time t_c; the end of the bubble can be bracketed probabilistically ex ante. — [Financial Bubble Experiment design (ar5iv 1005.5675)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1005.5675); [Bubble diagnosis and prediction (ar5iv 0909.1007)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0909.1007)
- Track record claimed by the group: ex-ante arXiv calls of the UK real-estate peak (mid-2004), US housing peak (mid-2006) and oil peak (July 2008); Sornette says about two-thirds of past predictions came true, attributing misses to exogenous shocks. — [Financial bubbles: mechanisms and diagnostics (arXiv 1404.2140)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.2140); [CXO summary of Sornette's own assessment](https://cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/when-stock-market-models-crash)
- Peer-reviewed supportive test (Sornette a co-author): Demirer, Demos, Gupta, Sornette (Quant. Finance 2019) find LPPLS confidence indicators capture ex ante Black Monday, dot-com and subprime periods on S&P 500/FTSE/Nikkei. — [Quant. Finance 19(5):843-858](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:19:y:2019:i:5:p:843-858). Self-assessment of the 2015 Shanghai bubble call. — [J. Investment Strategies](https://www.risk.net/ja/node/2427649). Reply to Greenwood et al.: "Bubbles for Fama from Sornette" (Sornette & Zhao 2021) reports good timing of regime shifts via superposed epoch analysis, stronger when the confidence indicator is larger. — [SFI working paper 21-94](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-21-94-bubbles-for-fama-from-sornette)
- Strongest evidence against: (i) CXO Advisory documents the S&P 500 LPPL forecasts of 2002-2005 missing with actual prices 50-60% above forecast minimums, and Sornette suspending forecasts and acknowledging the US predictions "have not been verified" (critic's characterization). — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/when-stock-market-models-crash); (ii) Bree, Challet, Peirano show the log-periodic functional form is "sloppy": fitted parameters shift substantially with small changes in sample, undermining out-of-sample use. — [Bree, Challet, Peirano](https://sonar.ch/documents/302953/files/cha_pas.pdf); (iii) Johansen (a former co-author) argues the "phase shift between first and second harmonics" in Sornette-Zhou lacks theoretical justification. — [arXiv cond-mat/0302141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0302141); (iv) Sornette and Johansen's rebuttal to Feigenbaum argues one cannot test a critical-point divergence after removing the data nearest the critical point. — [arXiv cond-mat/0106520](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0106520); (v) Greenwood, Shleifer, You (JFE 2019) find a sharp industry price run-up alone does NOT predict low future returns (partly vindicating Fama) but DOES raise crash probability far above the unconditional rate, and run-up features (volatility, turnover, issuance, path shape) forecast crashes. — [NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23191/w23191.pdf); [JFE 131(1):20-43](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:131:y:2019:i:1:p:20-43)
- Related "dragon-kings" claim (extreme events as outliers beyond the power law, generated by positive feedback, hence more predictable): evidence reported in financial drawdown statistics, city sizes (London), material rupture, epileptic seizures; but the authors admit no unique detection methodology, the earthquake case is contested, and critics call the predictability claim "wishful thinking". — [Sornette 2009 (arXiv 0907.4290)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4290); [Pisarenko & Sornette tests (arXiv 1205.1002)](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/1205.1002); [MIT Technology Review critique](https://www.technologyreview.com/2009/08/04/30396/how-dragon-kings-could-trump-black-swans/)

**Hawkes processes as "endogeneity / reflexivity" measures**
- Filimonov and Sornette (2012, Phys. Rev. E) fit a Hawkes process to E-mini S&P 500 mid-price changes and read the branching ratio n as the fraction of events triggered by other events (endogeneity); they report endogeneity rising from about 30% in 1998 to about 70% by 2007 (the search summary stated the direction inconsistently; the published claim is a RISE in reflexivity attributed to algorithmic trading — treat the exact endpoints as unconfirmed from the primary page). — [arXiv 1201.3572](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1201.3572); [SFI/CHF listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:chf:rpseri:rp1360)
- Hardiman, Bercot, Bouchaud (2013, EPJ B) re-fit the same E-mini data with a POWER-LAW kernel (exponent about -1.15 at short times, crossing over to a second power law) and find the kernel integrates to about 1 in every period 1998-2011: "markets are and have always been close to criticality", contradicting the claim that reflexivity increased. — [arXiv 1302.1405](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.1405); [EJoF 2022 review summarising the dispute](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/278329/files/Mark-Sila-Weber-EJoF-2022.pdf)
- Filimonov and Sornette (2013/2015, Quant. Finance) reply: calibrating Hawkes on mixtures of pure Poisson processes with regime changes yields spurious n near 1 when the true n is 0; power-law kernels with a few outliers and edge effects bias n upward; intraday seasonality and nonstationarity must be handled; hence HBB's "continuously critical" claim "cannot be supported". — [arXiv 1308.6756](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6756); [Quant. Finance 15(8):1293-1314](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:15:y:2015:i:8:p:1293-1314)
- Hardiman and Bouchaud give a fast branching-ratio approximation and list "a number of pitfalls" in estimating n via alpha/beta from an exponential fit. — [arXiv 1403.5227](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1403.5227)
- Commodity evidence: a Hawkes "flocking" study of WTI and RBOB gasoline futures 2007-2016 reports branching ratios of roughly 58-82% for WTI and 32-60% for gasoline, with asymmetric cross-market feedback that has narrowed over the decade. — [arXiv 2012.04181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04181). Filimonov presented an oil-futures "reflexivity index" (share of price changes from endogenous feedback) rising over the sample, not monotonically. — [IMF seminar slides](https://0-www-imf-org.library.svsu.edu/external/np/seminars/eng/2012/commodity/pdf/filimonov.pdf)
- No natural-gas-specific Hawkes study surfaced.

### Inferences (LOB predictions for a commodity futures book; UNTESTED unless noted)
- ZI model: for a given contract, the spread and short-horizon volatility should be predictable from the ratios of market-order, limit-order and cancellation rates alone; a change in those rates (e.g., around EIA releases) should move spread and volatility in the direction the ZI scaling says BEFORE any directional information is impounded. Mike-Farmer's caveat: expect the direction right and the magnitude/distribution wrong. Tested on LSE equities only.
- Latent book / square-root law: (a) impact of a metaorder in NG or CL should grow as Q^0.5-0.6 (the 0.6 end for large-tick contracts; NG at $0.001 tick on a ~$3 price is small-tick, CL at $0.01 on ~$70 is small-tick) — directly supported on CFM futures data by Toth et al.; (b) when a persistent one-sided flow STOPS, the price should revert part-way (to roughly 2/3 of peak within the day, per Bucci et al. on equities), and when it REVERSES the trajectory is non-trivial and model-specified (Donier et al.) — untested on commodity futures in anything found; (c) the visible book should be a poor predictor of impact relative to the flow itself, because visible liquidity is mostly HFT (Donier et al.'s premise). This is the published analogue to the project's "flow opposing price" reversal read: reversion after flow exhaustion is a mechanical-impact-decay prediction.
- Hawkes: a rising branching ratio in NG mid-price changes intraday would predict clustering/continuation of moves independent of news; but the Filimonov-Sornette vs Hardiman-Bouchaud dispute means any n estimate must be checked against a regime-switching Poisson null and both kernel families before it is read as "reflexivity".
- LPPLS: applied to a daily NG/CL trend it predicts a finite-time singularity; the Bree-Challet-Peirano sloppiness result means t_c estimates from a commodity run-up should be expected to be unstable to the sample window. Not a LOB-level tool.
- Minority game: predicts predictability hidden in higher-order statistics even when return autocorrelation is zero (Challet-Galla), which is a licence to test conditional, per-cell patterns rather than pooled autocorrelation; no direct LOB prediction.

### Gaps
- Could not access the full text of Donier et al. 2015 to extract the explicit formula for the price trajectory after an interrupted/reversed metaorder; the prediction above is qualitative.
- No empirical test of the latent-order-book reversal trajectory on commodity futures was found.
- No minority-game paper with an out-of-sample fit to real returns was found; the literature surfaced is theoretical.
- No independent, systematic scoring of the Financial Crisis Observatory's full ex-ante prediction record was found; evaluations are by the group itself or by partisan critics.
- The exact 1998 and 2007 endogeneity percentages in Filimonov-Sornette 2012 could not be verified from the primary page (arXiv fetch blocked).
- No natural-gas Hawkes/endogeneity study was found.

## 2. Ecology and evolution: adaptive markets, market ecology, agent-based models

### Takeaway
The ecological framing (strategies as species competing for the same flow, edges consumed as capital crowds in) is well-developed theoretically and reproduces stylized facts in simulation, but its empirical record is mostly "consistent with" tests of time-varying predictability; the one clean ecological prediction, density dependence of strategy returns, has been shown in a toy model, not measured on real strategy-capital data.

### Cited Findings

**Adaptive Markets Hypothesis (Lo 2004)**
- Core claim: efficiency is not all-or-nothing; it varies with the ecology of participants and the competition for resources; behavioural biases are heuristics adapted to past environments. — [Lo 2004 (MIT DSpace)](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/75362/1/Lo_Adaptive%20Markets.pdf)
- Supporting evidence: Neely, Weller, Ulrich find technical-trading profits in FX vary across regimes and decline as rules become known, "consistent with the AMH but not with the EMH". — [St. Louis Fed WP 2006-046](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf). Commodity futures: Coronado-Ramirez et al. (2015) test eight agricultural futures for nonlinear serial dependence under an AMH frame. — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/395/39535648006.pdf); [arXiv 1412.8017](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1412.8017). Shahid et al. find time-varying predictability in four major commodities, with heightened predictability during COVID-19. — [PMC8682897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8682897)
- Strongest evidence against: Alajbeg, Bubas, Sonje argue AMH does not yet yield a falsifiable theory of price formation, and that "empirical tests" of EMH/AMH are descriptions of statistical facts subject to the joint-hypothesis problem. — [hrcak.srce.hr](https://hrcak.srce.hr/file/116381). A City Journal review argues Lo fails to discredit EMH as thoroughly as implied. — [City Journal](https://www.city-journal.org/article/emotions-and-economics)

**Market ecology (Farmer 2002; Scholl, Calinescu, Farmer 2021)**
- Farmer (2002, Industrial and Corporate Change 11(5):895-953): a strategy's capital evolves like a species' population; under reinvestment of profits the capital-allocation model is equivalent to a standard population-biology model; combining value investing with trend following produces boom-bust cycles, excess and temporally correlated volatility and fat tails; the estimated timescale for the market to become efficient is years to decades. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:indcch:v:11:y:2002:i:5:p:895-953); [arXiv adap-org/9812005](https://arxiv.org/abs/adap-org/9812005v2)
- Scholl, Calinescu, Farmer (2021, PNAS 118(26)): in a model with noise traders, value investors and trend followers, strategy returns are strongly density dependent (depend on wealth invested in each strategy); pairwise relations can be competitive, predator-prey or mutualistic depending on wealth; at the efficient equilibrium all three are mutualistic; uses a community matrix and food-web tools; argues wealth shifts across strategies help predict mispricings, bubbles and crashes. — [arXiv 2009.09454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.09454v2.pdf); [PNAS listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nas:journl:v:118:y:2021:p:e2015574118); [phys.org summary](https://phys.org/news/2021-07-tools-ecology-financial.html)
- Strongest evidence against: the 2021 results are from a "stylized toy market model"; no measured strategy-capital data were used (per the sources above). The density-dependence prediction is therefore demonstrated, not tested.

**Agent-based models: Lux-Marchesi, Brock-Hommes, Santa Fe ASM**
- Lux-Marchesi (1999, Nature; 2000 IJTAF) and Lux (1995-98): chartist/fundamentalist switching with herding reproduces fat tails, volatility clustering and long memory; surveys describe these as nonlinear adaptive models that reproduce the key stylized facts. — [Hull repository survey](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1396196)
- Brock and Hommes (1998, JEDC 22(8-9):1235-1274): adaptive belief systems in which traders switch among forecasting rules by past performance; as the intensity of choice rises the model follows "rational routes to randomness" (bifurcations to cycles and chaos). — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:dyncon:v:22:y:1998:i:8-9:p:1235-1274); [1996 working paper](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:att:wimass:9621)
- Santa Fe Artificial Stock Market (Arthur, Holland, LeBaron, Palmer, Tayler 1997): the learning speed is the single parameter that moves the model from a near-rational-expectations regime (slow learning) to a complex regime (fast learning) where technical trading dominates and price/volume statistics resemble real data; Ehrentreich found a faulty mutation operator distorted the original bit distribution. — [Ehrentreich 2002](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/comp/papers/0209/0209001.pdf); [LeBaron overview (Tesfatsion mirror)](https://www2.econ.iastate.edu/tesfatsi/SFIStockMarketModel.LTOverview.pdf); [PLOS One 2018 replication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6005484)
- Estimation on data: a Norges Bank paper estimates a heterogeneous-beliefs ABM across asset classes and reports evidence for behavioural heterogeneity in expectations. — [Norges Bank WP 12/2017](https://www.norges-bank.no/contentassets/15bb5fc7cd1b453097aa713169373226/working_paper_12_17.pdf)
- Strongest evidence against: reviews note most stylized facts are only observable at high frequency and "there is still a gap between the heterogeneous agents models and observed market behavior"; reproduction of stylized facts is weak validation because many mechanisms reproduce the same facts. — [Stylized facts and ABM review (arXiv 1912.02684)](https://ideas.repec.org:443/p/arx/papers/1912.02684.html); [Hull survey](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1396196)

### Inferences (LOB predictions; UNTESTED)
- Density dependence: in a commodity book, the profitability of any given signal should fall as the capital running it grows, and the FOOTPRINT of the crowding should be visible as faster, more one-sided reaction to the signal's trigger (shorter lag, thinner far-side book). The project's observation that the futures->Kalshi lag is the edge is itself an ecological statement: the lag persists only while the capital arbitraging it is small. Prediction: the lag will shorten as the Kalshi market grows. (No source tests this on prediction markets.)
- Chartist/fundamentalist switching (Lux-Marchesi, Brock-Hommes) predicts regime alternation between trending (chartist-dominated, high volatility clustering) and mean-reverting (fundamentalist-dominated) days; the per-cell "regime x season x bucket" discipline is the natural test. In a LOB this should show up as the sign of flow autocorrelation flipping between regimes.
- AMH: expect edges in NG to be episodic and to decay after publication; a per-cell signal that "works on {X}, not {Y}" is the AMH-expected shape, not a failure.

### Gaps
- No study measuring strategy-level capital or "wealth invested per strategy" on real futures data to test density dependence was found.
- No ABM calibrated to a commodity LOB (order-level) surfaced; estimation work found is at daily asset-class level.
- Could not retrieve the primary Lux-Marchesi 1999 text (summaries only).

## 3. Reflexivity and feedback: Soros, hedging feedback, stops, deleveraging spirals, vol targeting, Minsky

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced feedback mechanisms are mechanical and measurable: option-dealer gamma hedging produces intraday momentum across 60+ futures (including commodities) that is strongest when dealers are short gamma, and stop-loss clustering produces measurable price cascades at round numbers in FX. Soros's reflexivity and Minsky's instability hypothesis have narrative force and some macro support but almost no clean intraday test.

### Cited Findings

**Soros's reflexivity**
- Core claim: participants' biased views affect fundamentals, which feed back into views (two-way feedback), producing boom-bust that is not a deviation from equilibrium but a self-reinforcing process. — [Soros excerpt (Investment Office)](https://www.investmentoffice.com/rc/doc/soros_5448.pdf)
- Evidence: Hommes (J. Economic Methodology 2013) reviews theory, empirics and lab experiments and argues non-rational "almost self-fulfilling equilibria" in positive-feedback systems have empirical relevance (learning-to-forecast experiments coordinate on trend-following in positive-feedback markets). — [Hommes (INET PDF)](https://ftp.ineteconomics.org/uploads/papers/HOMMES_Cars_EconMethodSoros.pdf); [INET page](https://ineteconomics.org/research/research-papers/reflexivity-expectations-feedback-and-almost-self-fulfilling-equilibria-economic-theory-empirical-evidence-and-laboratory-experiments)
- A 2026 arXiv study tests whether feeding LLMs Soros's framework improves directional S&P forecasts across the dot-com cycle vs another cycle; this is a test of forecasting behaviour, not of the mechanism. — [arXiv 2606.00061](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00061)
- Strongest evidence against: no standard econometric test of reflexivity was found; critics argue the theory is not sharply falsifiable. UNTESTED at the LOB level.

**Hedging feedback: portfolio insurance, gamma hedging**
- Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens (JFE 2021, 142:377-403): using intraday returns on over 60 futures on equities, bonds, commodities and currencies 1974-2020, the last 30 minutes' return is positively predicted by the return from prior close to the last 30 minutes, economically and statistically highly significant, reverting over the following days; linked to gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged ETFs; secondary summaries report momentum is much stronger when net gamma exposure is negative and absent when positive. — [JFE listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403); [working paper PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/)
- Barbon and Buraschi, "Gamma Fragility": large aggregate dealer gamma imbalances link to intraday momentum (negative gamma) and reversal (positive gamma), interacting with illiquidity; stronger for less liquid underlyings; the May 2010 flash crash was likely exacerbated by dealer positioning. — [Gamma Fragility](https://abarbon.com/papers/gamma-fragility); [St. Gallen record](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/261487). Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi, Moerke extend to single stocks and end-of-day dynamics. — [EOD paper](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)
- Strongest evidence against / limits: results are about intraday/EOD momentum, not "gamma squeezes" as events; vendor sites (SpotGamma etc.) make squeeze claims without published tests. — [SpotGamma commentary](https://spotgamma.com/wall-street-dealers-in-hedging-frenzy-get-blamed-for-volatility/)

**Stop orders and price cascades**
- Osler (NY Fed Staff Report 150): stop-loss orders cluster just beyond round numbers (take-profits on them); exchange-rate trends are unusually rapid when rates reach those levels; response to stop-losses is larger and longer-lasting than to take-profits; effects significant for hours, not days; motivated by the 11% USD/JPY drop of 7 Oct 1998; order-level data from one bank 1999-2000. — [NY Fed SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)
- Futures: Fett and McPhail (CFTC), "Stop Orders in Select Futures Markets" (E-mini S&P, 10-yr Treasury, WTI crude): stop orders execute more often on volatile days and "provide liquidity in the direction of the price move". — [CFTC research papers index](https://cftc.gov/About/EconomicAnalysis/ResearchPapers/index.htm?page=4)
- Flash crash: Kirilenko et al. document the 6 May 2010 E-mini cascade (hot-potato HFT volume, stop-logic halt). — [Kirilenko et al.](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/bitstream/1810/304244/1/KirilenkoFlashCrash.pdf)
- Strongest evidence against the retail "stop hunt / liquidity sweep then reverse" story: a practitioner review of Osler's data says the evidence leans toward CONTINUATION after a stop cluster is hit, not reversal, and a cited study finds "no evidence that ICT/SMC concepts work as literally taught" (low-reliability source, but no academic support for the reversal claim surfaced). — [thortradecopier review](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/is-stop-hunting-real)

**Liquidity spirals and deleveraging (Brunnermeier-Pedersen 2009, RFS 22(6):2201-2238)**
- Core claim: market liquidity and funding liquidity are mutually reinforcing; a "margin spiral" (margins rise with illiquidity) and a "loss spiral" (losses force sales) produce sudden liquidity dry-ups, commonality across assets and flight to quality. Evidence offered: S&P 500 futures margin spikes around 1987 and LTCM, prime-broker anecdote, NYSE specialist-capital commonality, Acharya-Pedersen flight-to-liquidity. — [Princeton PDF](https://www.princeton.edu/~markus/research/papers/Mkt_Fun_Liquidity.pdf); [RFS listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:6:p:2201-2238)
- Strongest evidence against / limits: the paper's evidence is illustrative; no independent out-of-sample test of the spiral mechanism surfaced in this search.

**Volatility targeting and risk parity**
- Harvey et al. (JPM 2018) find volatility targeting reduces drawdowns and tail losses and "effectively introduces some momentum" via the leverage effect. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- AQR "Not Risk Parity Funds" (2018): widely cited estimates of risk-parity selling in Feb 2018 overstated the strategy's size, equity weight and trading speed; the direction (sell when vol rises) is accepted, the magnitude disputed. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Not-Risk-Parity-Funds?aqrPDF=1); MSCI notes feedback potential without quantifying it. — [MSCI](https://support.msci.com/www/blog-posts/stress-testing-risk-parity/0862349677)
- Conclusion: a mechanism with accepted sign and disputed size; no intraday identification found.

**Minsky's financial instability hypothesis**
- Evidence: Davis, de Souza, Hernandez classify US nonfinancial firms into hedge/speculative/Ponzi regimes; the output gap raises the probability of moving to a more fragile regime only slightly; a secular rise in Ponzi finance; "Minskian dynamics do not seem to operate at business-cycle frequency". — [AEA 2017 paper](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/EFFYAa8N). A 2024 study of 1945-2023 US data finds debt ratios rose before recessions in 1945-1980s, weaker after. — [JRFM 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:gam:jjrfmx:v:17:y:2024:i:1:p:32-:d:1319300). Post-Keynesian reviews find stronger support for business-debt than household-debt cycles. — [PKWP 2513](https://postkeynesian.net/media/working-papers/PKWP2513.pdf)
- Relevance to intraday commodity trading: none demonstrated; macro-frequency only.

**Shiller's narrative economics**
- Critiques: Portes (IMF F&D) says the book makes little reference to empirical work and the central problem is causal direction (do stories drive events or accompany them?); Irish Times notes insufficient evidence for the "Trump narrative drove home prices" claim; Roos et al. argue empirical "narrative" papers mostly identify topics, not narratives. — [IMF review](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2020/03/book-review-narrative-economics-portes.htm); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/narrative-economics-incomplete-evaluation-of-impact-of-viral-stories-1.4118233); [Roos et al.](https://epflicht.ulb.uni-muenster.de/download/pdf/814012)

### Inferences (LOB predictions for commodity futures)
- Gamma hedging: on days when dealers are net short gamma in CL/NG options (large open interest near the money into expiry), expect the last-30-minute return to continue the day's move and the far-side book to thin as hedgers chase; on net-long-gamma days expect reversal and a replenishing far-side book. The Baltussen et al. result is already demonstrated on commodity futures at the daily/30-min level (sourced above); the LOB-level footprint (which side's ladder grows) is UNTESTED and is the natural extension of the project's "turning point = far-side liquidity recruitment" finding.
- Stops: expect clustering of stop (market-if-touched) executions just beyond round numbers in CL ($X.00/$X.50) and NG ($X.X00); on breach, expect acceleration and CONTINUATION for minutes-to-hours (Osler; Fett-McPhail), not the retail "sweep and reverse". Testable with the raw MBP-10 tape: stop executions appear as bursts of aggressive orders immediately after a round level prints.
- Liquidity spiral: a predictable footprint would be simultaneous widening across CL, NG, RB and metals books plus margin changes; the project could test cross-contract spread co-widening as a spiral proxy. UNTESTED.
- Vol targeting: predicts selling after a volatility spike regardless of direction, i.e. flow that opposes neither price nor fundamentals but tracks realised vol; in the NG book this would show as one-sided flow on the day after a vol jump with no news. Size disputed (AQR). UNTESTED intraday.

### Gaps
- No LOB-level (order-by-order) study of gamma-hedging footprints in commodity futures was found.
- No academic study of round-number stop clustering specifically in CME energy futures was found; Fett-McPhail covers WTI but I could not access the paper's figures.
- Brunnermeier-Pedersen: no independent out-of-sample test surfaced.
- Soros reflexivity: no accepted econometric test found.

## 4. Information-theoretic and entropy views

### Takeaway
Entropy-based tests give a usable operational definition of (in)efficiency — low Shannon entropy of the symbolised price/flow process = predictability — and transfer entropy is a directional lead-lag tool that beats correlation for direction of flow; but published applications to order-book STATE (as opposed to returns) are sparse, and the measures are sensitive to binning, lag choice, price staleness and zero returns.

### Cited Findings
- Shternshis, Mazzarisi, Marmi: treat low Shannon entropy as inefficiency; at the one-minute scale on a weekly basis the market is not efficient, though the signal is weak; price staleness and zero returns inflate apparent predictability and must be filtered. — [post-print](https://ricerca.sns.it/bitstream/11384/129004/5/post-print1-s2.0-S0960077922006130-main.pdf)
- A 2023 arXiv paper develops an entropy-based randomness test for ultra-high-frequency LOB data, examining persistence of order signs, return autocorrelation and volatility clustering; it finds a group of stocks where predictability comes from frequent changes in price direction (bid-ask bounce-like reversals). — [arXiv 2312.16637](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16637v3)
- Trading on entropy drops: a study of NYSE efficiency around meme stocks reports that trading on predictability patterns tied to entropy drops yields returns statistically above market averages; another warns a drop in sample entropy must be tested for significance since it may be noise consistent with efficiency. — [Springer 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10203-023-00427-9)
- Approximate/sample entropy vs Hurst: a thesis compares them as efficiency measures and trades low-entropy windows; daily/index data, not LOB. — [Charles University thesis](https://dspace.cuni.cz/bitstream/20.500.11956/85893/1/BPTX_2016_1_11230_0_513321_0_185468.pdf)
- Transfer entropy (TE): defined as the information X's past adds about Y beyond Y's own past; applications: Marschinski et al. (Dow -> DAX transmission); Fiedor on S&P 100 found causal links more prevalent than lagged synchronisation and the number of significant links falls quickly with lag; a 2021 tick-level study with TE, active information storage and multi-information finds information transfer rises in crisis periods; a 2019 EPJ Data Science paper builds symbolic-TE networks between individual traders from real buy/sell records. — [JSAI 2014 paper citing Marschinski](https://www.ai-gakkai.or.jp/jsai2014/webprogram/2014/pdf/238.pdf); [arXiv 2111.09057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09057v1.pdf); [EPJ Data Science 2019](https://www.doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-019-0188-6); [Financial Information Theory (arXiv 2511.16339)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16339)
- Microstructure lead-lag by correlation (not TE): a 2024 Frankfurt study finds the strongest non-contemporaneous relationships between transaction prices and midpoints and between order-book imbalance and midpoints. — [Frankfurt study](https://publikationen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/opus4/files/80803/4-5.pdf)
- Caveat: a 2026 Korean equity TE-network study finds networks stable across 1-20 day lags, read as slow structure rather than fast informational advantage, with no dominant leading investor type. — [arXiv 2603.20271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20271)
- Queue imbalance as a one-tick-ahead predictor (an information-in-the-book result): Gould and Bonart find imbalance improves binary classification of the next mid-price move by about 50-60% and probabilistic classification by 20-30% for LARGE-tick stocks, only 10-30% for small-tick. — [Gould & Bonart (arXiv 1512.03492)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.03492). Lipton, Pesavento, Sotiropoulos compute move probabilities conditional on imbalance for broker execution. — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/technology/trading-systems/2335976/trading-strategies-book-imbalance). Bechler and Ludkovski find top-level queue depth explains little at the meso-scale; deeper book shape matters more. — [arXiv 1708.02715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.02715). Queue depletion at the best quote is followed with high probability by a permanent price move (tick-size literature cited in the Lehalle signals paper). — [arXiv 1704.00847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00847)

### Inferences (LOB predictions; UNTESTED unless noted)
- "Touch-size entropy": the Shannon entropy of the distribution of touch (best-level) sizes is a state variable with no direct published analogue found; the nearest published results are (i) queue imbalance predicting the next tick (Gould-Bonart, equities, large-tick strongest) and (ii) entropy of the symbolised return/sign process as an efficiency gauge (Shternshis et al.). Prediction: low touch-size entropy (one dominant, replenishing size at the touch) indicates a single resting participant — an "absorption" state — and the next move should be AWAY from that side only when the size stops replenishing (queue depletion -> permanent move). This connects the entropy rule to the depletion result; it is untested in the literature found.
- TE for NYMEX->Kalshi lead-lag: TE is the right directional tool and should show a significant NYMEX->Kalshi link at seconds-to-a-minute lags and none in reverse; the Fiedor result (links die quickly with lag) predicts the TE signal collapses beyond the lag window. The 1-second NYMEX floor the project notes (undersampling) will bias TE downward, so a measured TE is a lower bound.
- Information in the tape vs about fundamentals: Bouchaud-Farmer-Lillo's claim that "much of the information processed by prices comes from supply and demand itself" (section 1) is the theoretical support for treating the tape as the primary information source and fundamentals as the trigger.

### Gaps
- No published measure of order-book STATE entropy (e.g., entropy of the depth profile or of touch sizes) as a predictor was found; this appears to be novel ground or at least not surfaced here.
- No TE study of futures-to-prediction-market lead-lag was found.
- Compressibility-based efficiency tests (e.g., Lempel-Ziv) were not surfaced within the search budget.

## 5. Flow- and inventory-based asset pricing

### Takeaway
"Flow is the fundamental" has the strongest empirical record of any alternative framework at the horizons that matter here: order flow explains >50% of daily FX changes and beats a random walk out of sample (Evans-Lyons); dealer balance-sheet capacity forecasts energy futures returns in and out of sample (Etula); the aggregate stock market's price multiplier to flows is about 5 rather than the efficient-markets zero (Gabaix-Koijen), with Bouchaud showing this is the same object as daily price impact.

### Cited Findings
- Evans and Lyons (JPE 2002; NBER w7317): macro models rarely exceed R2 of 10% in sample and lose to a random walk out of sample; adding interdealer order flow gives R2 above 50% for daily DM/$ and yen/$ changes and significantly better short-horizon out-of-sample forecasts than a random walk; $1bn of net dollar purchases moved DM/$ by about 1 pfennig (one abstract version says about 0.5%). — [NBER w7317 PDF](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7317.pdf); [Georgetown PDF](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/evansm1/wpapers_files/orderflow.pdf)
- Intermediary asset pricing: He and Krishnamurthy (AER 2013) model risk premia during crises as driven by intermediary capital. — [BFI PDF](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/intermedap-new1.pdf). Adrian, Etula, Muir show covariance with broker-dealer leverage shocks explains the cross-section of 65 equity and 6 Treasury portfolios. — [NY Fed SR625](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr625.pdf). He, Kelly, Manela (JFE 2017) find significantly positive prices of risk for the intermediary capital factor in all asset classes including commodities, options, CDS, FX and bonds. — [JFE published version](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/jfepublishedversion.pdf)
- Commodity-specific: Etula (J. Financial Econometrics 2013, 11(3):486-521; NY Fed SR406): broker-dealer risk-bearing capacity is "an important determinant of risk premia in commodity derivatives markets"; a limits-to-arbitrage model predicts premia fall as sector relative leverage rises, strongly supported in 14 commodity markets, with "particularly strong forecasting power for energy returns, both in-sample and out-of-sample". — [NY Fed SR406](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr406.pdf); [JFEC listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v11y2013i3p486-521.html)
- Hedging pressure decomposed: Kang, Rouwenhorst, Tang (JF 2020, 75(1):377-417) show hedging pressure mixes speculators' liquidity demand and hedgers' insurance demand, which move expected futures returns with OPPOSITE signs; short-term position changes are driven by noncommercials' liquidity demand, long-term by commercials' hedging demand; commercials' liquidity-provision gains roughly offset the insurance premium they pay. Marechal (JFM 2023) replicates with a panel and examines financialisation. — [JF listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:75:y:2020:i:1:p:377-417); [working paper PDF](https://www7.uc.cl/economia/finance_uc/docs/conferences/11th/Rouwenhorst-Tale%20of%20two%20premiums.pdf); [Marechal 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v43y2023i5p580-614.html)
- Inelastic Markets Hypothesis (Gabaix-Koijen): the aggregate equity price-impact multiplier M is estimated at about 5 (range 3-8 across specifications) vs the efficient-markets benchmark M = 0, identified with granular instrumental variables; attributed to mandate-constrained index/pension/insurance funds. — [NBER conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f141240.pdf); [2026 paper summarising the range](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05277)
- Bouchaud's microstructural reinterpretation: M of order unity-to-5 is consistent with daily-scale price impact; M increases with the stock's volatility and decreases with the traded fraction of market cap; the two literatures (Kyle lambda / square-root impact and GK's macro multiplier) measure the same thing. — [arXiv 2108.00242](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2108.00242); [Scientia profile](https://www.scientia.global/professor-jean-philippe-bouchaud-the-inelastic-market-hypothesis-explaining-the-origins-of-financial-fluctuations/)
- Strongest evidence against: no direct published critique of the GK multiplier surfaced (the search returned only reconciliations); for Evans-Lyons, the main limitation is that interdealer order flow is not public in real time and the result is in-sample dominant with the out-of-sample margin at short horizons only (per the abstracts above).

### Inferences (LOB predictions; UNTESTED unless noted)
- Dealer inventory as driver: in CL/NG, the sign of aggressive flow over the last N seconds should predict the next move with an R2 far above any fundamental variable at that horizon (Evans-Lyons analogue); the project's dip_imb_level result (direction 7%/93% monotone on strong flow) is this prediction confirmed in-house, not yet published externally.
- Intermediary capacity: on days when dealer balance sheets are constrained (e.g., after a vol spike), impact per unit flow (Kyle lambda) in energy books should be higher and the book thinner (Etula's premia-vs-leverage result translated to microstructure). Testable as lambda vs a broker-dealer leverage proxy. UNTESTED at intraday level.
- Inelastic markets: a known mandate-driven flow (index roll windows, e.g., the GSCI/BCOM roll days) should move the front/second-month spread with a multiplier well above zero, i.e. flows without information move price and do not fully revert. UNTESTED here; roll-window studies exist in the literature but were not surfaced in the budget.
- Kang-Rouwenhorst-Tang: a short-horizon liquidity-demand flow from speculators and a long-horizon insurance-demand flow from hedgers have opposite return signs; a LOB classifier that separates them (burst aggressive vs patient resting) should see opposite forward returns. UNTESTED.

### Gaps
- Exact GK abstract and identification details could not be pulled from NBER (fetch blocked); the M = 5 figure comes from the conference PDF and secondary summaries.
- No published study of intraday Kyle lambda vs intermediary capital in energy futures was found.
- Index-roll flow studies (Mou; Bessembinder et al.) exist but were not reached within the budget.

## 6. Auction and game-theoretic views: continuous vs batch auctions, microstructure invariance

### Takeaway
Budish et al. show the continuous limit order book mechanically creates latency-arbitrage races (about one per minute per symbol on the FTSE 100, about $5bn/year globally) that widen spreads; Kyle-Obizhaeva's invariance supplies dimensionally-grounded scaling laws (bet size, bet arrival, impact vs dollar volume and volatility) that are "economically close" on 400,000+ portfolio-transition orders and hold intraday on the E-mini with a W proportional to N^(3/2) law replicated by Bouchaud's group.

### Cited Findings
- Budish, Cramton, Shim (QJE 2015): the HFT arms race is a market-design artefact of continuous-time serial processing; frequent batch auctions (uniform-price sealed-bid double auctions every ~1 second or less) would convert competition on speed into competition on price, narrowing spreads and deepening books. — [UMD profile](https://econ.umd.edu/publicationprofile/2087); [NBER conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)
- Aquilina, Budish, O'Neill (QJE 2022): on FTSE 100 message data, latency-arbitrage races occur about once per minute per symbol; the global equity prize is about $5bn/year; eliminating latency arbitrage would cut the cost of liquidity by about 17%. — [AEA preliminary paper](https://benny.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/Tt353Bff); [Budish, Lee, Shim on exchange incentives](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/12988/files/Theory-of-Stock-Exchange-Competition-and-Innovation.pdf)
- Strongest evidence against / limits: no results from a live frequent-batch-auction venue were found; the exchange-competition paper argues exchanges have weak incentives to adopt it.
- Kyle and Obizhaeva (Econometrica 2016, 84(4):1345-1404): invariance hypotheses — bet size and transaction costs have specific testable relationships to dollar volume and volatility, derived from the idea that "business time" runs at the bet-arrival rate; tested on 400,000+ portfolio-transition orders; "even though our statistical tests usually reject the invariance hypothesis, the results are economically close to those implied by the theory". — [ECTA PDF with supplement](https://pages.nes.ru/aobizhaeva/Kyle-Obizhaeva-ECTA-2016-Invariance-with-Supplement.pdf); [Econometrica listing](https://econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2016/07/01/market-microstructure-invariance-empirical-hypotheses)
- Futures: Andersen, Bondarenko, Kyle, Obizhaeva, "Intraday Trading Invariance in the E-mini S&P 500" (Jan 2008-Sep/Nov 2011): return variation per transaction is log-linear in trade size with slope -2, across the diurnal pattern and across days; motivated by but "not directly implied by" the 2016 invariance principles. — [NES WP 272](https://www.nes.ru/files/Preprints-resh/WP272.pdf); [AEA 2017 draft](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/bNFQsR3f)
- Replication/critique: Benzaquen, Donier, Bouchaud ("Unravelling the trading invariance hypothesis") restate the law as risk exchanged W scaling as the 3/2 power of the number of trades N (invariant I = W/N^(3/2)), find it holds "remarkably well" at 1-minute intervals across time and contracts, and offer a microstructural (not business-time) explanation. — [CFM PDF](https://www.cfm.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/303-2016-Unravelling-the-trading-invariance-hypothesis.pdf); [arXiv 1602.03011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03011)

### Inferences (LOB predictions for commodity futures)
- Continuous double auction: in NG/CL, every public information event (EIA storage/petroleum status, NYMEX tick) should be followed within microseconds-to-milliseconds by a race in which the stale quotes at the touch are picked off before they can be cancelled; the footprint is a burst of aggressive executions against resting orders immediately at the event time followed by a spread widening. The NYMEX->Kalshi lag the project trades is the same race at a far slower clock (seconds), and Budish's logic says the lag survives only because Kalshi's participants are not yet running the race. Empirically demonstrated on FTSE equities; UNTESTED on commodity futures and on Kalshi.
- Invariance: for NG at a given dollar volume V and volatility sigma, the typical bet (metaorder) size, the number of trades per unit time and the price impact per bet should scale with V and sigma as the invariance laws dictate; intraday, the per-trade return variance should fall as trade size rises with slope -2 in logs (E-mini result). This gives a dimensionally-grounded NULL for "how much should the book move per contract traded", so that a deviation (impact per contract above the invariance prediction) is a measurable "surprise" state. UNTESTED on NG/CL specifically; E-mini only.

### Gaps
- No invariance test on energy futures was found.
- No live batch-auction venue data were found.
- Exact invariance exponents (bet arrival vs W) were not confirmed from the primary text; stated qualitatively.

## 7. Practitioner frameworks: auction market theory / market profile, order-flow and footprint, Wyckoff, absorption/exhaustion, liquidity hunting

### Takeaway
None of the practitioner frameworks has independent, published out-of-sample evidence; the testable kernels inside them (value-area reversion, delta/price divergence, absorption as hidden-liquidity replenishment, stop clusters) map onto academic results that are at best partially supportive (iceberg detection, OFI-reversal) and in one case contrary (post-sweep continuation, not reversal).

### Cited Findings
- Market Profile / Auction Market Theory (Steidlmayer, CBOT 1980s): the "value area" is defined by analogy to a normal distribution as the central 70% of TPO/volume around the point of control (+/-1 sd, 68.3%); the "80% rule" (if price opens outside and re-enters the value area and holds, it traverses the area 80% of the time) originates in Dalton Capital Management's Profile Reports (1987-91); practitioner text claims AMT is "based on empirical studies" but none are cited; the first academic Market Profile course (Drinka, WIU, 1987) was teaching, not research. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile); [NexusFi value area](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/value-area); [NexusFi 80% rule](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/80-percent-rule); [AMT practitioner text](https://www.scribd.com/doc/55884921/Auction-Market-Theory)
- No peer-reviewed empirical test of value-area or 80%-rule claims surfaced. UNTESTED.
- Wyckoff accumulation/distribution: an evidence-review site finds academic literature thin (one non-top-tier JISEM paper), no published win rate, no documented adoption by systematic funds, and quants regarding it as pseudo-scientific; backtests are vendor-driven (LuxAlgo, TradingView scripts); it warns against dropping failed springs from a backtest. — [evidencefinance.neocities.org/wyckoff](https://evidencefinance.neocities.org/wyckoff); [LuxAlgo Wyckoff](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/wyckoff-method/). UNTESTED.
- Order-flow / footprint "delta divergence" (price higher high while cumulative delta lower high): vendors define it as a condition not a trigger and warn session-wide divergence is not necessarily a reversal; feed quality (true tick vs synthetic delta) matters; no vendor reports out-of-sample performance, trade counts or significance. — [LuxAlgo delta divergence](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/delta-divergence/); [ATAS divergence workflow](https://learn.atas.net/volume-basics/volume-analysis/divergence-scale-and-workflow). UNTESTED by independent study.
- Academic analogues to "absorption" (large resting/hidden liquidity eating aggressive flow): iceberg orders' price impact rises with order size and depends on the executed fraction (liquidity, not information, explanation); detected icebergs strongly attract market orders ("facilitating the search for latent liquidity"); books with icebergs at the best quotes have greater visible depth and narrower spreads; hidden orders have no relevant impact but raise trader aggressiveness when revealed. — [Frey & Sandas (CFR Cologne)](https://www.cfr-cologne.de/download/workingpaper/cfr-09-06.pdf); [NYU hidden-orders paper](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/26511/2/FIN-04-004.pdf). On CME futures, Zotikov and Antonov (Quant. Finance 2021) detect native icebergs from resting-volume vs trade-size discrepancies and synthetic icebergs from limit orders arriving shortly after a trade, then predict total hidden size with a Kaplan-Meier estimator, validated out of sample on full-depth data. — [QF 21(11):1977-1992](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v21y2021i11p1977-1992.html); [ar5iv 1909.09495](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.09495)
- Academic analogues to "exhaustion / divergence": Cont, Kukanov, Stoikov show short-interval price changes are linear in best-quote order flow imbalance (OFI) with slope inversely proportional to depth. — [arXiv 1011.6402](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402). Deeper-level OFI adds explanatory power (Xu, Gould, Howison; Cont, Cucuringu, Zhang). — [arXiv 1907.06230](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06230); [arXiv 2112.13213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.13213v2). A 2026 crypto preprint (not peer-reviewed) finds short-horizon reversal concentrates after bars where aggressive flow moved price and grows with the flow, while book-depth measures add nothing beyond flow: "what conditions the reversal is the aggressive flow, not any hole it leaves in the book" — i.e., compensation for absorbing flow. — [arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888)
- Liquidity hunting / stop runs: see section 3 (Osler; Fett-McPhail; continuation not reversal).

### Inferences (LOB predictions; UNTESTED unless noted)
- Market Profile: the testable kernel is "price that re-enters a high-volume node reverts toward its POC"; stated as a conditional distribution of forward moves given distance from the session POC, per cell. No published result.
- Delta divergence = the practitioner's name for the dipole DIVERGENCE read (aligned_flow < 0). The academic analogues say: (i) OFI predicts the concurrent move linearly (Cont et al.), so a price move WITHOUT matching OFI is anomalous and (ii) reversal after flow-driven moves is compensation for liquidity provision (crypto preprint). Prediction for CL/NG: a new price extreme on lower aggressive imbalance should revert more often than one on higher imbalance, with the effect scaling with the imbalance shortfall rather than with visible depth. The project's "book/dipole/exhaustion = noise for SHAPE, magnitude confounds" finding is consistent with the crypto preprint's "flow, not the hole in the book".
- Absorption: the measurable version is iceberg replenishment at the touch (Zotikov-Antonov detection on CME). Prediction: a touch level that repeatedly refills after trades (native or synthetic iceberg) will attract more market orders (Frey-Sandas) and the move AWAY begins only after the refill stops (queue depletion). This is the published scaffolding for the project's touch-size-entropy rule and "far-side liquidity recruitment" finding.
- Liquidity hunting: expect continuation for minutes-to-hours after a stop cluster is hit (Osler; Fett-McPhail), so a "sweep then reverse" rule should FAIL on average; reversal, if any, must be conditioned on something else (e.g., the sweep occurring on low OFI).

### Gaps
- No independent out-of-sample backtest of Market Profile, Wyckoff or footprint delta-divergence rules was found; all evidence is vendor or anecdotal.
- No academic paper using the words "absorption" or "exhaustion" as defined LOB measures was found; the nearest are iceberg-replenishment and OFI-reversal results.
- Could not access the Zotikov-Antonov full text for detection accuracy figures on CME energy contracts.

## 8. Novel syntheses: markets as measurement devices, prices as queues of disagreement, conservation laws, and the dipole/divergence idea

### Takeaway
No published framework was found that states "markets as measurement devices", "prices as queues of disagreement" or "conservation laws of the order book" as a formal, tested theory; the closest rigorous relatives are the latent-order-book reaction-diffusion picture (where the conserved quantities are the latent buy/sell intentions that are annihilated at the price), the long-memory-of-order-flow result (positions must be unwound, so flow is persistent), and the OFI/impact literature. The project's dipole/divergence read has published analogues in the OFI-reversal and impact-decay results but no direct published test.

### Cited Findings
- Reaction-diffusion as a conservation picture: in Donier et al. the latent book is two densities (buy intentions, sell intentions) that diffuse, are deposited/cancelled and ANNIHILATE where they meet (the price); the single emergent parameter L is the local slope of latent liquidity; prices decompose into mechanical (transient) and informational (permanent) parts. — [arXiv 1412.0141](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.0141v3); Toth et al.'s V-shaped latent supply/demand profile with liquidity vanishing at the price is the same object. — [arXiv 1105.1694](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1105.1694v3)
- Positions as the conserved quantity: Bouchaud, Farmer, Lillo argue large positions must be built/unwound incrementally over long periods, making signed order flow a long-memory process; efficiency then requires an asymmetric liquidity response and slow impact decay. — [arXiv 0809.0822](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.0822v1.pdf)
- Divergence between flow and price as a reversal precursor (published analogues): (i) price change is linear in OFI with slope ~1/depth (Cont-Kukanov-Stoikov) so price moving without OFI is anomalous. — [arXiv 1011.6402](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402); (ii) reversal after flow-driven moves grows with the flow and is not explained by depth (crypto preprint, unrefereed). — [arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888); (iii) metaorder impact reverts to ~2/3 of peak after the flow ends (Bucci et al.). — [arXiv 1901.05332](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05332); (iv) the dipole-divergence 65% reversal finding and its cost-floor history are the project's own (in-house, provisional; see CLAUDE.md).
- Markets as near-critical "measurement" systems: Hardiman-Bercot-Bouchaud (kernel integral ~1 in all periods) and Challet et al. ("marginally efficient" at the phase transition) are the published forms of the idea that the market sits at the edge where a small input can be amplified or damped. — [arXiv 1302.1405](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.1405); [CCMZ 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v1y2001i1p168-176.html)
- Ecology as the frame for "edges are consumed": density dependence of strategy returns (Scholl et al.) and the years-to-decades efficiency timescale (Farmer 2002). — [arXiv 2009.09454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.09454v2.pdf); [Farmer 2002](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:indcch:v:11:y:2002:i:5:p:895-953)

### Inferences (LOB predictions; UNTESTED)
- Conservation: in a commodity futures book the conserved quantities are (a) open interest (every long has a short), (b) each participant's inventory, and (c) in the latent-book picture, latent intentions until annihilation. Prediction: a move that is driven by a transient-inventory holder (HFT/market maker) must be unwound within the holder's horizon and so reverts (mechanical impact); a move driven by a hedger/producer changing OPEN INTEREST does not need unwinding and so persists (informational). The order-level tape can tag whether aggressive flow adds to or reduces open interest (new vs offsetting) only with position data, but trade-size and persistence proxies (metaorder detection) are the published route. UNTESTED.
- Prices as queues of disagreement: the standing visible book is literally the queue of resting disagreement with the current price; the latent-book theory says most disagreement is NOT in the visible queue. Prediction: the visible far-side queue grows (recruitment) when latent intentions are drawn into the visible book by the price approaching them — the project's turning-point finding — and the reaction-diffusion model would make the growth rate proportional to L times the distance moved. UNTESTED quantitatively.
- Measurement device: a release (EIA) is a measurement of the latent book's slope L: impact per contract in the first second after the release is an estimate of 1/L. Tracking L across releases gives a state variable for "how much the book will move per unit surprise"; invariance (section 6) predicts how L should scale with volume and volatility, so residuals are the novel content. UNTESTED.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper framing markets explicitly as "measurement devices" or prices as "queues of disagreement" was found; these phrases do not appear to correspond to an established literature.
- No published paper on "conservation laws" of the order book beyond the reaction-diffusion annihilation picture was found.
- No published test of flow-vs-price divergence as a reversal signal on futures LOB data was found; the only direct analogue is an unrefereed crypto preprint.
- The disagreement/volume literature (Hong-Stein, Harris-Raviv) was not reached within the search budget and is noted as a missing cross-reference.
