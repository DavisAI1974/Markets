## CONTEXT — the desk, the target, and the rules

## 1. WHAT THIS DESK IS

Two people: Greg Davis (founder, 20+ years NYMEX/ICE energy trading, sets direction) and an AI
engineer. One market: **Henry Hub natural gas.** Three product lanes off one forecast — Kalshi
daily event contracts (KXNATGASD, settles 17:00 ET), NYMEX futures (NG), and NG options.

**Unit convention, used throughout and worth memorising:** one NG contract is 10,000 MMBtu, so
**$100 of "day move" = 1.0 cent of gas ($0.01/MMBtu)**. When we say a forecast erred by 592, we
mean 5.9 cents of gas.

**The product is a PRICE CURVE, not a number.** This was settled in the previous session and it
reorganised the programme. The intraday path is the deliverable — its legs, its turns, its peaks
and valleys — not a single end-of-day move. "Curve" is the trader's noun for the pricing curve; it
is jagged, and that is fine.

**The method is ANALOG RETRIEVAL, and the ordering matters.** We do NOT search history for a
lookalike and read its outcome. We:

1. project what market conditions will be on the target session;
2. reason from those conditions to how the session should **behave**;
3. retrieve a past trading day whose **shape** matches the behaviour we already concluded;
4. re-anchor its **level** — level is the ONLY free parameter;
5. monitor continuously against the live tape and adjust or scrap.

**The analog does not do the forecasting. It renders it.** It supplies real traded intraday texture
instead of a smooth guess. Three rules hang off this and they are load-bearing:

- **Amplitude is a REJECTION TEST, not a knob.** If you must stretch the retrieved day's amplitude
  more than slightly, you have the wrong analog — go find another. One free parameter cannot be
  tortured into fitting.
- **The scrap signal is SLOPE, not level.** Level errors are correctable by re-anchoring; slope
  errors are not. Differencing removes level automatically, so no fitting step is involved.
- **The adjustment loop IS the product.** Greg: "no trader has this information, you just have to
  adjust faster and better than everyone else."

The historical record we have walked (23 two-week blocks, G1-G23) is therefore **a library build,
not a scoring exercise**: each past session stored with the conditions present and the curve it
traded. The *reasoning* for why a day traded as it did is the retrieval key — which means **a day
we scored well but explained wrongly is a corrupt library entry**, and a play that is right for the
wrong reason is as serious as one that is wrong.

## 2. THE MEASURED CONSTRAINTS — do not propose anything that violates these

These are measured, not assumed. Several were expensive to learn.

**2.1 Horizon. Directional LEVEL forecasting dies at 5-7 days, measured on Henry Hub price itself.**
Raw meteorological skill runs to day 10-12, but the price relationship to weather dies at 5-7. The
gap is the market having already priced the forecastable part. Any horizon claim built on the day-10
number is wrong by a factor of two for trading purposes.

What survives past day 7: **dispersion** (variance is additive and most of the vol curve is
deterministic — Samuelson decay, calendar seasonal, scheduled events), **the forward calendar**
(maintenance, LNG in-service, expiry, rolls — dated forward, no horizon limit), and **the revision
process** (the run-to-run forecast delta is what actually moves price at day 6-15). So: forecast the
revision, not the level; price the width, not just the centre; trade the calendar, which is not a
forecast at all.

**2.2 The dimension budget — the hardest constraint we have.** Analog theory and nearest-neighbour
theory give the same exponent: `L = k / r^d`. For a decent normalized match at a single retrieval,
d=3 needs ~1,000 sessions, d=4 needs ~10,000, d=10 needs 10^10. **Our library is a few hundred to a
couple thousand sessions, so our matching dimension is about 3, at most 4.** Condition on ten things
simultaneously and the retrieval returns a day provably no closer than a random one — and it will
still return one, confidently, with a magnitude attached. Two corollaries: retrieving more analogs
*costs* dimension, and depth does not rescue you (operational analog systems saturate near four
years of archive and then degrade as the world changes underneath the library).

This is why a **regime label** matters so much to us: it collapses the match to d≈1-2, which is the
only dimension our library size permits.

**2.3 We are currently losing to a zero-change forecast.** Across seven blocks, blind mean absolute
error is ~1.13x the mean absolute actual move — i.e. worse than forecasting "no change" in six of
seven blocks. Direction is 24 of 50, indistinguishable from a coin. The apparent improvement over
time (939 down to 592) tracked realized volatility falling (799 to 457); the market got quieter and
we read it as progress. Those runs were degraded in known ways and ran an architecture we are
replacing, so this is a **benchmark, not a verdict** — but it is the bar. **Never state an error
number without naming the benchmark it beat.**

**2.4 We cannot yet measure skill, because the system cannot decline.** The output contract requires
a numeric magnitude, so a forecaster with no read still emits a number and constructs a rationale for
it. Measured across 50 days carrying a declared confidence: exactly one was declared high, and the
confidence field does not discriminate accuracy at all. A forced call and a confident one are
indistinguishable downstream. **Building a computable NO CALL is a measurement prerequisite, not a
trading nicety.**

**2.5 What we are up against.** Gas is the least machine-penetrated liquid energy contract — 24.5%
average HFT share against WTI's 52.1%, ~19% of volume with a human on both sides. But the Kalshi gas
ladder is roughly **$3,600 of notional with eleven at-the-money trades in 6.5 hours** and a 3-cent
spread. Our clearest claimed edge is uncontested largely because it is too small to contest. We are
not a latency business, not a data-acquisition business, and not a breadth business. What we can be
is a calibrated-distribution business in a contract where the weather-to-burn *conversion* is the
hard part and is not commoditized.

## 4. HOUSE RULES FOR YOUR ANSWERS

These are our standing operating rules. They are not stylistic.

1. **Name the benchmark before the result.** Any claim of predictive value must say what it beats —
   zero-change, seasonal-naive, last close. "Improves MAE" with no benchmark is not a finding.
2. **Falsification first.** For every mechanism you propose, state what evidence would kill it. A
   claim that points the same way whether it fires or not is worthless to us; we have retired plays
   for exactly this.
3. **Per cell, never pooled.** A pooled rate is a cancellation artifact. If something works on a
   subset of conditions, say "works on X, not Y" — partial coverage is a result, not a failure.
4. **Three distinct states for evidence, never collapsed:** FOUND (with the citation), SEARCHED AND
   FOUND NOTHING (a real result), NOT SEARCHED (an open task). If you did not verify something, mark
   it unverified in place rather than at the end.
5. **A finding with no instance beside it is not a finding.** Put the supporting case in the same
   sentence or the next one, never in a separate section and never by reference.
6. **Nothing local.** Every path, endpoint or file you name must be something anyone can reach — a
   public URL, a bucket, an API route. Never a path on somebody's machine.
7. **No emojis or special symbols** anywhere in your output. Plain text and plain tables.
8. **Distinguish measurement from vendor marketing.** Much of what circulates about gas positioning
   and skew is vendor commentary with no published statistics behind it. If a claim has no study, say
   "no published statistics; vendor claim only."
