HOW THE VARIABLES BECOME A TRADEABLE CONDITION
  greg s114: 'And how all of the variables create market conditions that get traded on.' 'It really helps when you know how all the pieces are supposed to fit together and what the end goal is.'
  read this as a CHAIN not a checklist: Your state serves dozens of fields. They are not independent predictors to be weighed and averaged - they are stations on ONE causal path from physical weather to a price you can trade. Knowing where a field sits on that path tells you what it can and cannot say, and stops you using a supply-side fact to answer a demand-side question.
  the physical chain:
    - WEATHER sets LOAD. Degree-days are the forcing, but the response is an asymmetric SLOPE, not a switch: mild weather kills demand hard, heat and cold add demand but only some of the time. A hill SLOPES, it does not gap. Weights must be SEASONAL - summer gas demand is roughly half power burn, winter is dominated by res/comm heating.
    - LOAD is met by a STACK: nuclear, coal, gas, with wind/solar/hydro as non-regulating forcings that arrive whether you want them or not. GAS IS THE ONLY TERM THAT REGULATES, so anything that removes or adds a forcing lands on gas. That is why wind rising 62% cut burn 4.2 Bcf/d on 0629 while the degree-days came in exactly as forecast.
    - THE FORCINGS ARE NOT ONE TERM. Wind and solar are seasonally ANTI-correlated (wind peaks in spring/autumn, solar at the solstice) - never sum them. Hydro is an INVERTED U whose BOTH tails are bullish, because past a river stage the water goes through the spillway instead of the turbines. Coal is a startup-constrained RAMP on a 1-2 week commitment, not a dial.
    - BURN FOLLOWS GENERATION, NOT LOAD (D38). A balancing area with no gas generation still creates gas demand next door. Interchange only cancels at the US48 level.
    - BURN plus heating plus exports minus production sets the STORAGE trajectory, which the weekly EIA print adjudicates. That print is the scheduled moment the market's belief gets marked.
    - AND THE PRICE IS AT ONE POINT. Henry Hub is a single physical location near Erath, Louisiana - not a national index. The object is demand HH can SEE.
  the market chain that turns that into a MOVE:
    - Fundamentals set a SLOW backdrop - a block-trajectory lean, never a day-level side or timing signal. Fundamentals tell you where the river is going, not what happens in the next two hours.
    - POSITIONING decides how violent the response is. The same fundamental news moves price far more when one side is crowded and trapped, because the move is forced unwinding, not opinion. That is why COT, open interest and the options pin map are on your state.
    - FLOW is the nowcast - it tells you which side is actually being pressed right now, and it is the one channel that speaks before price confirms. But flow is a NUMERATOR: it says how hard someone is pushing, not whether the balance can absorb it.
    - STRUCTURE AND CALENDAR decide WHEN it is delivered - the print, the expiry, the roll, the opex, the weekend seam. A correct read on the wrong clock scores as a miss.
    - LIQUIDITY decides how much price a given push buys, and it MOVES: once a void is filled, the same flow stops moving price (k3 collapsing 0.302 -> 0.026 -> 0.018).
  so a market condition is a CONJUNCTION: This is why the plays are conditional and multi-limb rather than single-variable. A tradeable condition is a forcing plus a stack that cannot absorb it plus a positioning that must react plus a clock that delivers it. Any one limb alone is noise. Your job at step 1 is to recognise WHICH conjunction your day is standing in - that is the classify-then-simulate move, and it is why the day-class comes before everything else.
  the end goal in one line: Turn that recognised conjunction into a shape, the shape into a level, and the level into a position someone would actually stake money on - repeatably enough that the library can do the recognising for us.
