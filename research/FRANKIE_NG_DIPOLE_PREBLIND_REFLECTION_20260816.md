## What I thought dipole was before this reveal

My existing brain already treated dipole/order flow in two distinct ways:

1. `direction.flow_nowcast` treats signed dipole imbalance as a strong held-leg side / direction nowcast when the signal is sufficiently strong. It is a nowcast overlapping the nascent leg, not a from-flat forecast.
2. `timing.subsecond_reversal_exhaustion` treats dipole exhaustion/collapse toward neutral as a turn-timing hypothesis: the collapse, not merely a crossing, is the indication that the prior leader is weakening. The brain explicitly kept this as an under-evaluated execution-layer hypothesis rather than established blind doctrine.

The brain also contains adjacent concepts that matter for interpretation: continuation-collapse at a mature turn (`exit.recruitment_reversal`), flow-vs-price absorption (`direction.flow_conviction_sign_gate`, `direction.absorption_is_reversal`), the distinction between true absorption and a flowless withdrawn-book reprice (`flow.flowless_reprice_is_not_absorption`), and price-free absorption proxies / resting-program effects (`flow.price_free_absorption_proxy`, `flow.resting_program_inverts_aggressor_tilt`).

So before this experiment I thought of dipole primarily as a DIRECTION/turn-timing toolkit with an exhaustion arm that was promising but incompletely evaluated.

## What the reveal changed

I no longer think the useful object is one scalar dipole reading.

The revealed families make dipole look like a TRAJECTORY / STATE MACHINE:

**pre-flip morphology -> post-flip authority state -> price-horizon response**

The pre-family says something about the setup. The post-exhaustion path says whether that setup's directional authority persists, collapses, or transfers to a broader horizon. The relevant price relationship can change with scale, so one family should not be assigned one universal duration/direction label.

### Family A

A looks like the dominant/base exhaustion regime, but the important information is the state it enters after t0. The revealed A-persistent state retains same-side dipole/flow authority and has substantially more 3t/5t/8t runway than A-fast-collapse. A-fast-collapse loses or reverses authority quickly and has much shorter runway. This is the strongest replicated reveal finding and it is consistent across all four days.

My brain crosswalk for A is continuation-collapse versus retained flow authority. The blind question is not merely 'is this A?' but 'does A retain authority or fail after the flip, and does existing context make that persistence trustworthy?'

### Family B

B looks more local/impulsive. Its revealed directional relationship is strongest at the small/local scale and degrades as the price-leg scale broadens. B-persistent versus B-collapse is also clearest around the local 3t horizon.

I do not interpret B as simply a short family. My working read is that B may be a LOCAL FLOW AUTHORITY state whose information decays before the wider market structure is resolved. Brain concepts around continuation failure, absorption, resting-program inversion, session phase and regime may explain when that local signal should or should not propagate.

### Family C

C looks more like a transition / broader-structure state. Its small immediate 2t/3t leg can disagree with dipole, while alignment increases on the broader 5t/8t/13t structure. Persistent C strengthens that broader relationship and carries longer 5t/8t runway than collapse C in the reveal sample.

I do not interpret that as 'C means long.' I interpret it as evidence that the immediate micro-leg can be the wrong scale for the state the dipole is describing. That makes C particularly interesting for the blind test: it may distinguish a local counter-move from the broader move that ultimately owns the structure.
